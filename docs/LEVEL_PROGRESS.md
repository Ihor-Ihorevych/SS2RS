# Level progress: kills, secrets, time, checkpoints and the end-of-level summary

`ss2_game::level_progress` (T09-023) is the **bookkeeping of one level**: how many enemies there were and how many died, which secrets were found, how long it took, which checkpoint the player is at, and the numbers the end-of-level screen shows. It is pure logic (time is a count of simulation ticks, never a clock; no randomness; no `HashMap`), so the same events always give the same state, byte for byte. **Nothing in the game calls it yet** (see the end): it has only been exercised by its own tests.

## What the spec says, and what I chose

The reverse-engineering notes say little, so most of the rules below are **mine**:

| Topic | Spec (`docs/re/`) | Chosen here |
|---|---|---|
| Secrets | `CSecretEntity` (110 in the retail worlds) has a message, a stay time, a speech sound, "happy music", a state and a **finder handle**; scripts fill it with `MarkFulfilled` (111 uses) and it raises `Found` and `Ended` (`level-logic.md`, `level-logic-api.md`). The total per level is the number of such entities (`CWorldInfoEntity.en_cenSecrets` is a container of them). | Secrets are numbered `0..total`; each is found once; the first finder keeps the credit; the total comes from the level. |
| Checkpoints | **Chapters** are the respawn points: `CChapterInfoEntity` in a **linear chain** (1 to 12 per world), one active at a time, started by a macro action; a chapter holds the start inventory; the world info has `TeleportPlayersToCurentChapter` and `macResetWorldToCurrentChapter` (`level-logic.md`, `help-digest.md`, `player-rules.md`). | A checkpoint is a chapter: reached **in order, once each**; reaching one stores a snapshot. Skipping one is refused. |
| Kills | `AllKilled`/`OneKilled`/`GroupKilled` events of spawners; spawners create enemies while the level runs, so the total is **not known at load** (`ai.md`, `level-logic.md`). | Enemies are **registered** as they are created; the total is what was registered, never below the kills. |
| Score | Per kill the victim's `mpp_slScore` (41 of 126 characters give 0, bosses 5,000), `macAddScore` on the world info (6 uses), extra life every 10,000, serious score x5 (`game-modes.md`, `player-rules.md`). | The caller passes the points; the module adds them per player and keeps an uncredited share (`macAddScore` has no player). The 10,000-point extra life and the x5 multiplier are **not** here. |
| What a retry keeps | **Not in the data.** The world is reset to the chapter; no rule about counters or the clock is stated. | See below. |
| Par time, the summary screen | **Not in the data** (the original's statistics screen is not described). | The summary has the numbers a stats screen would need; par time is optional. |

## The rules

- **Kills.** `register_enemies(n)` when a spawner creates `n` enemies; `record_kill(by, score)` when one dies. `by` is a player slot or `None` (a trap, another enemy). The team count `kills()` is the **sum** of the per-player credit and the uncredited kills, so the credit always adds up to the team count. `kills_total()` is the registered number but **never below the kills** (a kill that was never registered raises it, so "12 of 11" cannot be shown). A kill for a slot that does not exist is refused.
- **Secrets.** `find_secret(id, by)` returns `New` once and `AlreadyFound` afterwards; the second finder gets nothing. An id at or past the level's secret count is refused.
- **Time.** `advance(ticks)` adds to the clock; it saturates and does nothing after `finish()`. `summary(tick_hz)` converts to **whole milliseconds, rounded down** (`ticks * 1000 / hz`, exact in 128-bit arithmetic, saturating at `u64::MAX`); a tick rate of 0 is refused. The caller decides what advances the clock (it should not advance while paused or in a cutscene).
- **Checkpoints.** `reach_checkpoint(index, payload)`: the next one in order is `New` (the snapshot is stored); one already reached is `AlreadyReached` and changes nothing (the **first** payload is kept; a retry walking over an old checkpoint does not overwrite it); a later one is `OutOfOrder`; one at or past the level's count is refused. The snapshot is the counters (enemies registered, kills, score, secrets with their finders, per player) and the tick. **Only the last checkpoint's snapshot and payload are kept.**
- **The payload** is opaque: `CheckpointPayload { version: u32, bytes }`. The module never looks inside; it limits the size (`max_payload`, default 1 MiB, never above 16 MiB) and refuses a larger one without reaching the checkpoint. The version is the caller's: the caller compares it on restore and decides what an old one means.
- **Restore.** `restore()` goes back to the last checkpoint reached, or to the level start if none:
  - **counters go back to the snapshot**: kills, the enemies registered, score, secrets found and who found them. This is the choice that keeps the numbers true: restoring the world brings the enemies killed after the checkpoint back to life, so keeping their kills would count them twice, and a secret found after the checkpoint is back in the world to be found again (it is `New` again; one found before the checkpoint stays found);
  - **checkpoints already reached stay reached** (the next new one is still the next in order);
  - **the clock keeps running** by default (the time spent on the failed attempt counts, as on a speedrun timer); `RestoreTime::Rewind` puts the clock back to the checkpoint's tick (0 at the start) for a mode that wants that;
  - `restores()` counts the retries and is in the summary.
  The caller restores the *world* from the returned payload; this module does not touch it.
- **Co-op.** Counters are the **team's**; the credit per player (`kills`, `secrets` found, `score`) is kept separately and always adds up to the team numbers (plus the uncredited share). Up to 64 slots. A restore restores everyone.
- **Finish.** `finish()` freezes the level: later changes are refused with `Finished` (the clock is ignored), so the summary read afterwards cannot move.
- **Score** is optional: with `scoring: false` points are ignored and the summary has `score: None` (kills still count).

## The summary

`summary(tick_hz)` gives a `LevelSummary`: `kills`/`kills_total`, `secrets`/`secrets_total`, `elapsed_ticks`/`elapsed_ms`, `par_ms` and `within_par` (at or under the par time counts as within), `score`, `restores`, `checkpoints`/`checkpoints_total`, and one `PlayerSummary` per slot. `kill_percent()` and `secret_percent()` give whole percentages rounded **down** and capped at 100; with **nothing to kill (or no secrets) they are `None`**, so a screen can print "-" instead of an invented 100 %.

**Mapping to the UI.** The end-of-level screen (T10-020, `ss2_ui::session_screens`, which may not be on `main` yet) has its own `LevelStats`. This crate does not depend on `ss2-ui`; the game code that shows the screen builds the UI's value from the summary: kills and total from `kills`/`kills_total`, secrets from `secrets`/`secrets_total`, the time from `elapsed_ms`, the par time from `par_ms`, the score from `score`. The exact field names are the UI's to define; check them against `ss2_ui::session_screens` when wiring (T09-023b).

## Saving the state

`to_bytes()` writes everything (setup, clock, counters, the checkpoint snapshot and its payload): `"S2LP"`, a format version (`u16`, 1), little-endian integers, secrets in ascending order, and the CRC-32 of everything before it. The same state gives the same bytes. `from_bytes()` refuses a wrong checksum, magic or version, a truncated or over-long input, a setup outside its limits, a flag that is not 0 or 1, a count or length that does not fit the bytes or the limits, secrets that are repeated, out of order or out of range, a finder that is not a player, a score in a mode without score, more checkpoints reached than exist, a snapshot that disagrees with the count, a checkpoint from the future, and a payload over the limit. **A different version is refused; there is no migration.** This is *not* a save file of the game (see below): it is the progress model's own state, for the join-in-progress snapshot or a future save to embed.

## How it was tested

**Forty unit tests** (`level_progress_tests.rs`), all deterministic and device-free. They cover: the limits of the setup at each boundary; kills credited to a player or to nobody and the team count equal to the sum; the total never below the kills; a kill or score for a slot that does not exist; every counter saturating instead of wrapping (kills, registered enemies, score, the clock); score with and without a scoring mode; a secret found twice (and by a second finder), an unknown secret, an unknown finder; time converted at 1, 30, 60, 1,000 and 2,000 Hz, rounded down, a tick rate of 0 refused, a clock of `u64::MAX`; par time at exactly the limit; percentages with zero totals (`None`), rounding and capping; checkpoints in order, twice, out of order, beyond the level and with no checkpoints at all; the payload limit at its boundary (a refused checkpoint is not reached; an empty payload is fine); the first payload kept on a repeat; restore with none, to a checkpoint (counters, secrets found again, secrets found before staying found, per-player credit), twice in a row, and the clock under both `RestoreTime` rules; a finished level refusing everything; the same events giving **byte-identical** state (and the order in which secrets are found not changing the bytes); CRC-32 against the standard check value; round trips of a played level and of four setups, fresh and busy (including 64 players and 4,096 secrets); a restored copy behaving like the original; **every prefix of the bytes refused and every byte flipped refused**; damage re-sealed with a correct checksum (magic, version, players, each flag, trailing and missing bytes); every inconsistency a reader must catch (12 hand-built states); secrets repeated or out of order; a hostile payload length; 2,000 rounds of pseudo-random damage and garbage, which must not panic; and the error texts.

**Mutation check of my own code: 71 deliberate one-line breakages** of `level_progress.rs` (a script replaced exactly one piece of text, ran the module's tests, and put the file back each time): wrapping instead of saturating arithmetic (six of them), `>` for `>=` and back at every limit, a removed check, a flipped condition, a counter not reverted, the clock rule inverted, a wrong CRC polynomial, a reader that accepts a bad flag. **Result: 68 killed at once, 2 survived, 1 did not compile (I re-wrote it).** The two survivors were a par flag and a checkpoint flag that was not 0 or 1: the file was still refused, but *further on* and for another reason (the bytes shifted), so my "any error" assertion could not tell; I added one test that checks **which** error is given for each, and both (and the re-written one) are now killed. **Final: 71 killed, 0 survived, 0 equivalent.** That says my tests notice those breakages, not that the rules are the original's: the mutants are my own idea of what could go wrong.

Not run: any fuzz target for `from_bytes` (the pseudo-random test above is a smoke test, not coverage) and any data-gated test (there is no game data in it).

## Not covered (follow-up T09-023b)

- **No world snapshot.** The checkpoint payload is opaque bytes the caller supplies; **nothing captures the game world into it and nothing restores a world from it** (the world snapshot is `ss2_sim::save` / T08; applying a restore to entities, players' inventories and scripts is gameplay that does not exist yet).
- **Nothing calls it.** No spawner registers enemies, no enemy death records a kill, no `CSecretEntity` calls `find_secret`, no chapter entity calls `reach_checkpoint`, no simulation tick calls `advance`. Wiring needs the entities (T09-010, T09-018, T09-021) and the level-flow (T09-025).
- **No save file and no quick-save rules.** `to_bytes` is not wired into `ss2_sim::save` or any slot; when a checkpoint is written to disk, how many, and what co-op does when one player quick-saves are open (`docs/SAVES.md`).
- **No UI.** Nothing draws the summary; the mapping to `LevelStats` is described, not written (and `ss2_ui::session_screens` may not be on `main` yet).
- **Rules that are my guesses**, to be checked against the original when its behavior can be observed: counters reverting to the checkpoint, the clock continuing, checkpoints being strictly in order (a script that jumps a chapter would be refused), the total of enemies being what was registered. The retail data does not say.
- **Not here:** the extra life at 10,000 points, the x5 serious-score power-up, per-difficulty score, the statistics of multiplayer modes (frags), objectives (`SMissionObjectiveEntity`), a par time for any real level (there is no source for one), and a cap on how many times a level may be retried.
- **Replication.** The state is deterministic and serialisable, but nothing replicates it to clients and the byte format has no schema hash tied to the entity classes.
