## Summary

- A level's **environment** is a handful of single entities, almost all optional: **one sky cube** (58 of 59 worlds), **0 to 5 haze volumes** (65 in 45 worlds), **0 to 2 bloom entities** (34 in 33 worlds), **fluid volumes** (23 in 15 worlds), force fields (38 in 7), shakers (12 in 7), environment damagers (23 in 2).
- **There is no separate fog or water entity.** Fog-like and haze effects are **haze volumes** (a box with a colour and four numbers); water is a **fluid hull** (a box volume with a density) plus materials that use the water shader (`mtr.md`: 52 `CWaterShaderArgs`); the post effects in the data are **bloom** and camera **shakers** only. Underwater overlays, fades and damage flashes are not stored in the worlds (T04-031).
- Every effect entity has a **runtime renderable** that **copies** the member values of its property record; the record is what an editor shows and a reader should use.

## Evidence

`tools/re/wld_env_verify.py` and `wld_entity_schema.py --match …` (read-only, `SS2_DATA_DIR`) over the retail install with all patches:

- **Sky cube:** the record's size equals the renderable's size and its colour equals the renderable's colour in 58 of 58, and its `.4` equals the renderable's `CCubeRenderable.2` in 58 of 58; 55 skies name an external `.tex` and have face textures on the renderable (five faces, a sixth in 11); 3 skies have none.
- **Haze:** 65 of 65 records are matched member for member by the renderable (table below); **bloom:** 34 of 34; **fluids:** the record's density and second value equal the hull's in 23 of 23.
- **Sky cube faces (T01-405):** `tools/re/sky_faces_verify.py` decodes the 53 skies whose face textures are format-8 (two more use pre-format-8 "legacy" textures, `tex-legacy.md`, out of scope here) and compares every pair of face edges. Four members form a consistent four-face ring (edge errors 4.2–5.0 on a 0–255 scale, in 40–41 of 53 skies with both faces, against 17–23 for the two ring diagonals that are not really adjacent); a fifth member borders all four ring faces (errors 2.6–10.7 in 43–44 of 53) and a sixth, present in only 10 of the 53, borders two of the four convincingly (2.7 and 6.0) and is never adjacent to the fifth (55.8): the fifth and sixth members are the cube's top and bottom, the fifth is the one that is (almost) always present.
- **Sky cube placement (T01-409):** `tools/re/wld_env_verify.py` reads `CCubeBackgroundEntity.en_qvPlacement` on all 58 skies. 44 of 58 (76%) carry a **non-identity rotation**; every one of the 58 (the 44 rotated and the 14 identity) is either the identity or a **pure rotation about +Y** (heading only — the renderable's up axis is never tilted, i.e. pitch and bank are always exactly zero, within 1e-3 on the matrix). Of the 44 non-identity headings, only 6 are within 0.5° of a multiple of 90°; the rest spread over nearly the whole -180°..180° range (`1_3_village` itself is -110.0°, matching this task's starting observation exactly). The **position** component of `en_qvPlacement` is never (0, 0, 0) on any of the 58 skies and has no relation to the level's playable area (values from a few metres to tens of thousands, e.g. -37,346 on the Y axis in `dm_chanolin`): it looks like wherever the level editor happened to drop the entity, not a value the renderer could sensibly use to position a cube that must always surround the camera.

## Sky: `CCubeBackgroundEntity`

```
CCubeBackgroundEntity (58 worlds, one each; record CCubeBackgroundProperties)
  .1  -> CCubeRenderable      .3 -> CSimpleBackgroundViewerRenderable
  en_fSize     FLOAT 64 (56 worlds), 32 (1), 6 (1)        en_fViewerY  FLOAT -6.66 .. 4.52     en_qvPlacement QuatVect     en_bActive 1
CCubeBackgroundProperties
  .1  -> CTexture: an external .tex (55; null in 3)   .6 COLOR (6 distinct values; 0xFFFFFF in most)   .2 = en_fSize   .3 = en_fViewerY   .4 FLOAT 0 or 1   .5 BOOL
CCubeRenderable
  .1 size,  .2 FLOAT 0 or 1,  .3 COLOR,  .10 .11 .12 .13 .15 -> CTexture (five faces), .14 -> CTexture (a sixth face, set in 11 skies)
CSimpleBackgroundViewerRenderable   .1 FLOAT 5.22 or 55.68
```

A sky is a **cube of up to six textures drawn around the viewer** at size 64, with a colour tint and a vertical viewer offset: the textures are named per level (`mdigbo_village_bckg_0002.tex`, `kronor_snowmnt_00.tex`, `lavaarena_01.tex`, …). The one world without a sky entity is `dockingbay`.

### Which face member is which direction (T01-405)

| `CCubeRenderable` member | Compass name | Engine axis | Present |
|---|---|---|---|
| `.10` | North | **−Z** | 53 of 53 decoded skies |
| `.11` | West | **−X** | 53 of 53 |
| `.12` | South | **+Z** | 53 of 53 |
| `.13` | East | **+X** | 53 of 53 |
| `.14` | Floor (down) | **−Y** | 10 of 53 (11 of 55 counting the legacy-texture sky below) |
| `.15` | Ceiling (up) | **+Y** | 53 of 53 |

The compass names (**medium**, from the engine binary) come from `SeriousEngine::CCubeRenderable::AutoSetTextures` and the helper it calls, `txuMakeCubeFileNames` (`Engine.dll`, read-only, never copied into this repo): given one base texture name ending in a recognised letter, it builds six file names by appending the literal suffixes `"N"`, `"W"`, `"S"`, `"E"`, `"C"`, `"F"` (or, for a base name ending in a digit, `"1"`…`"6"`) — the letters stand for **N**orth, **W**est, **S**outh, **E**ast, **C**eiling and **F**loor — and assigns them, in that order, to the renderable's six texture-pointer members in ascending offset, which is member order `.10, .11, .12, .13, .14, .15`.

The **ring order** (N next to W next to S next to E, not N next to S) and the **top/bottom identity** (one member touches all four ring faces and is almost always present; a second member, rarely present, touches two of the four convincingly and is never adjacent to the first) are **high confidence**, found independently from the decoded face pixels (`tools/re/sky_faces_verify.py`, evidence below) without reading the binary at all — and the two results agree exactly: North→West→South→East→North is the ordinary counter-clockwise compass cycle. Which engine axis each compass direction is remains a **convention, not a measurement**: `−Z` for North (forward) and `+X` for East follow the standard right-handed assignment (`GEOMETRY.md`: right-handed, Y up, −Z forward; `Right = Forward × Up`, so facing North along −Z puts East at +X) and match the engine's own documented heading convention elsewhere (`behavior-validity-terms.md`: heading 0 is along −Z, a positive heading turns towards −X, i.e. West — consistent with North = −Z, East = +X in a right-handed frame).

### How the face images tile (the orientation rule)

A face texture decodes as in `tex.md`: rows top to bottom, columns left to right. The renderer draws all six faces with the one identical unit-square UV mapping (`CCubeRenderable::RenderObject`, binary, **medium**: only the bound texture differs per face in the drawing loop), so the only per-sky choice is how the artist drew each image to meet its neighbours; `tools/re/sky_faces_verify.py` finds this **from the pixels**, independent of the binary, by matching every pair of face edges (top, bottom, left, right; read forward or reversed) across every sky that has both faces:

| Faces (member) | Edge of the first | Edge of the second | Mirrored | Agreeing skies | Mean edge error (0–255) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| North–West (`.10`–`.11`) | North's left | West's right | no | 41/53 | 4.2 |
| North–East (`.10`–`.13`) | North's right | East's left | no | 40/53 | 4.3 |
| West–South (`.11`–`.12`) | West's left | South's right | no | 40/53 | 4.2 |
| South–East (`.12`–`.13`) | South's left | East's right | no | 40/53 | 5.0 |
| North–Ceiling (`.10`–`.15`) | North's top | Ceiling's bottom | no | 44/53 | 3.0 |
| West–Ceiling (`.11`–`.15`) | West's top | Ceiling's left | no | 44/53 | 2.6 |
| South–Ceiling (`.12`–`.15`) | South's top | Ceiling's top | **yes** | 43/53 | 10.7 |
| East–Ceiling (`.13`–`.15`) | East's top | Ceiling's right | **yes** | 44/53 | 8.9 |
| North–Floor (`.10`–`.14`) | North's bottom | Floor's top | no | 7/10 | 2.7 |
| East–Floor (`.13`–`.14`) | East's bottom | Floor's right | no | 4/10 | 6.0 |

North–South, West–East (the two ring diagonals) and Ceiling–Floor never matched consistently (mean error 17.0–55.8, and the best-matching edges differed from sky to sky instead of agreeing): they are the cube's three **opposite** pairs, which never share an edge, exactly as a cube's geometry requires. West–Floor and South–Floor did not match convincingly either (best errors 27–30, no consistent winner among only 10 skies with a floor face at all) and are **not checked**; by the symmetry of the other three ring faces (each borders the ceiling along its own top edge) they are expected to be West's bottom–Floor's left, no flip, and South's bottom–Floor's bottom, mirrored, the same pattern that the ceiling shows one quarter and one half of the way around the ring from North.

**Reader rule:** place North (`.10`) at −Z, West (`.11`) at −X, South (`.12`) at +Z and East (`.13`) at +X, Floor (`.14`) at −Y and Ceiling (`.15`) at +Y. Going around the horizon the order is North, West, South, East (**not** North, East, South, West); each ring face's left edge continues unflipped into its western neighbour's right edge (so reading the four images in texture-row order, left to right, continues the horizon moving west). Each ring face's top edge continues into the ceiling: unflipped for North and West, mirrored for South and East (and, expected by symmetry, into the floor the same way).

### `.1`: which face it is

The record's own texture (`CCubeBackgroundProperties.1`, `mdigbo_village_bckg_0002.tex` in the world above) is **not a seventh, separate image**: in all 55 named skies it is byte-identical to one of the renderable's own face textures — North (`.10`) in 38, Ceiling (`.15`) in 13, South (`.12`) or East (`.13`) in 2 each (`tools/re/sky_faces_verify.py` cross-check against `wld_env_verify.py`'s record reader, **high**, 55 of 55). It reads as a left-over "representative" pointer, perhaps what a level editor's sky picker shows; a reader only needs the renderable's own six (or five, or in 3 worlds, zero) face members and can ignore `.1`.

### Size, viewer height and colour

`CCubeBackgroundProperties.2` equals the entity's `en_fSize` (the cube's half-extent: **64** world units in 56 of 58 skies, 32 in one, 6 in one) and `.3` equals `en_fViewerY` (**−6.66 to 4.52**, 0 in most): together they place a cube of that size centred on the viewer but offset vertically by `en_fViewerY`, so the cube moves with the camera horizontally but not (fully) vertically — the usual trick to keep a sky cube's horizon level while letting the player fly without the sky "sliding". `.6` is the ARGB colour tint copied onto the renderable (`0xFFFFFF`, i.e. no tint, in most skies; 6 distinct values across 58 worlds) — multiplied over every face's pixels, by analogy with the bloom colour (`CBloomEntity`, above) which is read the same way. `.4` (FLOAT, 1 in 54 of 58, 0 in 4) equals the renderable's `.2` in all 58 skies (same high-confidence record-to-renderable copy as `.2`→size, `.3`→viewer Y and `.6`→colour); its meaning is not named in the data. `.5` (BOOL, 1 in 16 of 58) does not consistently equal anything on the renderable this task checked and remains **not established** (open question, unchanged from T01-044).

### Placement: the cube is rotated, never translated (T01-409)

`CCubeBackgroundEntity.en_qvPlacement` is **not editor-only**: the binary (`SeriousEngine::CCubeRenderable::RenderObject` and `SeriousEngine::CSimpleBackgroundViewerRenderable::AddToRendering`, decompiled `Engine.dll`, read-only, never copied into this repo) builds the drawn cube's model matrix from a quaternion using the full, standard quaternion-to-matrix formula (all four components, the same construction already used by `tools/re/wld_root_verify.py` and `tools/re/skl_verify.py` for every other placement in this codebase) — so the renderer is **structurally applying the full rotation**, not a reduced heading-only angle, and the sky's placement is genuine input to rendering, read every frame, not a value the engine only shows an editor. The quaternion consumed there is the renderable's own placement, which (as for every renderable in this engine, `wld-root.md`) is kept in sync with the owning entity's placement — here `en_qvPlacement` — through the ordinary entity-to-aspect sync, not something special-cased for the sky.

The same function (`AddToRendering`) composes that rotation against the **inverse of the current camera's rotation**, while its translation is taken from the camera/view's own position fields, not from the renderable's own placement position. Net effect, medium confidence: the drawn cube's **orientation follows `en_qvPlacement`'s rotation and stays fixed in world space** regardless of which way the camera looks (the standard skybox trick: cancel the camera's rotation so the object's own rotation is what remains on screen), while its **position always tracks the camera**, not the entity's placement position — consistent with the position evidence above (every one of the 58 skies has a non-zero, seemingly arbitrary `en_qvPlacement` position that cannot be what actually places the cube, since the cube must always surround the player). `en_fViewerY`'s own combination with the camera position (e.g. whether it is added before or after this step) is script logic (`CCubeBackgroundEntity` is a scripted entity, not in the native decompiled tree) and was **not traced further**: treat it as an additive Y offset on top of the camera position, per the data evidence already in the previous section.

`CCubeRenderable.1` (the size) is read directly by both `GetBoundingBox` and `RenderObject` to build the drawn cube's extents (medium confidence, binary) — it is not just stored, consistent with the already-high-confidence record/renderable equality. `CCubeRenderable.2` (the record's `.4`) enters the same extent formula as the size in `RenderObject` (medium-low: based on reading default-constructor values to tell its storage offset apart from neighbouring members, not on a full trace of the formula) — that reads as a second geometric term, not a plain on/off visibility switch as previously guessed; its exact role is still **not established**.

**Reader rule:** apply `en_qvPlacement`'s **full rotation** to the cube (rotate all six face quads by it around the camera); **ignore `en_qvPlacement`'s position** (and `en_fSize`'s role is unaffected: it still sets the cube's half-extent around the camera, offset vertically by `en_fViewerY`, as in the previous section). Applying only the yaw (heading) component would give bit-identical results for every one of the 58 retail skies, because none of them have a pitch or bank component — but that is a fact about this data set, not about what the renderer does, so a reader that aims to match the engine should not rely on it.

### Faces with no texture, and the world with no sky at all (T01-413)

T01-070 opened `CCubeRenderable::RenderObject`'s per-face draw loop but could not conclusively tie its per-face pointer checks to texture presence within its budget. Reading the whole function (`Engine.dll`, binary, **medium**) settles it: for each of the six face members in turn, a **null** texture pointer does **not** skip the face — the renderer binds an engine-internal, always-present white texture (`gfxBindTexture`'s `gfx_hWhiteTexture`) and falls into the *same* draw path a textured face uses: texture wrapping, `gfuUseSimpleShader`, `gfuConstantColor` with the renderable's own tint colour (`CCubeRenderable.3`, forced fully opaque — the colour's own alpha byte is discarded here, the opposite of the haze colour's alpha, which is read) and a draw call for that face's quad. White multiplied by a colour is that colour, so a face with no texture renders as **a flat quad in the sky's own tint colour** (`0xFFFFFF`, plain white, on most skies) — never literally nothing.

**One face is checked a second way.** The floor (`.14`) is the only member with an extra condition: when the floor's own pointer is null *and* the ceiling's (`.15`) is not, the loop skips the floor face entirely — no draw call at all, confirming reader invariant 6's existing guess (an open bottom) as measured binary behaviour rather than an inference. When the floor is null **and the ceiling is also null** — the three skies with no face texture at all (`1_4_approachingcity`, `dm_cockaleekie`, `dm_hole`; their record's own texture, `.1`, is null too) — that exception does not fire either, and the floor gets the same flat-tint quad as every other face. So those three skies render as a **fully enclosed, flat-tinted cube** around the viewer, not an open shape and not nothing.

`dockingbay`, the one world with no sky entity at all, never reaches this function in the first place: there is no `CCubeRenderable` to render, so nothing stands in for a sky, and the view shows whatever the renderer clears the background to. The product's existing behaviour for a sky-less level (a clear colour) needs no change; only the three texture-less-but-present skies do.

**Reader rule:** draw a sky face with a null texture pointer as an opaque quad filled with the sky's own tint colour (default white), in the same place a textured face would sit — except the floor, which is omitted entirely (no quad, open bottom) whenever the ceiling face is present, and otherwise (no ceiling either) also gets the flat-tint quad like every other face. Render a world with no sky entity exactly as before (no sky, background clear colour) — nothing here changes that case.

## Haze: `CHazeEntity`

`CHazeEntity` (65 in 45 worlds; 14 worlds have none, 31 one, 11 two, one each three, four and five): `.1` → `CHazeEffectRenderable`, optional parent entity (5 are attached). The property record and the renderable hold the **same numbers** (checked on all 65):

| Record `CHazeProperties` | Renderable `CHazeEffectRenderable` | Range |
|---|---|---|
| `.1` COLOR | `.3` | any (e.g. `0x2a383d` in the village world) |
| `.2`, `.3`, `.4` FLOAT | the extents of the box `.1` (`Box3f`) | 10 to 10,000 (the volume's size) |
| `.5` | `.2` | 0 to 999.8 |
| `.6` | `.4` | −300 to 500 |
| `.7` | `.5` | 0 to 5,900 |
| `.10` | `.7` | 0 to 2,500 |

So a haze is **an axis-aligned box volume with a colour and four numbers**; the renderable is `CRenderingDomain.9` (`wld-lighting.md`). `environment-levels.md` lists every haze of every level so the values can be compared with the original's look.

### Role and formulas of `.5`, `.6`, `.7`, `.10` (T01-070)

`CHazeEffectRenderable::AddToRendering` (`Engine.dll`, read in full, **medium**, binary) runs once per haze per frame, gated by a global render-state bit (the haze feature can be switched off entirely, like a video option). It builds **one draw-command entry** per haze and appends it to a shared, growable per-frame list (order = append order = `CRenderingDomain.9`'s list order, confirming reader invariant 3 below); nothing in `Engine.dll` combines several entries into one effect — that step, and the pixel-level maths that turn one entry into a screen colour, is not reachable without `Shaders.dll` (T01-020, still not decompiled: see the "Bloom" section below for the same gap).

- **`.5` (renderable `.2`, 0 to 999.8) is an edge-fade distance in world units**, clamped at render time to at least 0.001 (so it is never truly zero). The box (`.2`/`.3`/`.4`, the renderable's `Box3f`) is inset by this distance on every face to make an inner box (if a box's extent on one axis is shorter than twice the fade, that axis's inner bound collapses to the box's own mid-point, so a very thin haze cannot invert itself). The renderer then takes the **camera position in the box's own local, rotated frame** and computes its (clamped, per-axis) distance to that inner box — zero while the camera is inside it. The haze's **per-frame intensity scalar is `fade = 1 − clamp(distance / .5, 0, 1)`**: 1.0 while the camera is more than the fade distance inside the box, ramping linearly down to 0.0 exactly at the box's own outer surface, and staying 0.0 outside. With `.5` authored as 0 (clamped to 0.001) this is, in effect, a hard cut at the box boundary rather than a ramp. This scalar is the fourth field of the draw-command entry (after colour and `.6`/`.7`); it is the only place the camera's position enters the haze's CPU-side computation.
- **`.6` (renderable `.4`, −300 to 500) and `.7` (renderable `.5`, 0 to 5,900) are a pair, and `.7` is clamped against `.6`**: every frame the renderable overwrites its own `.7` with `max(.6, 0.01, .7)`, so `.7` can never end up below `.6` or below 0.01 even if the level data said otherwise. In all 65 instances of the retail data `.7` ≥ `.6` already, so the clamp never changes an authored value here — it reads as a safety net on the renderer's side, consistent with `.6`/`.7` being a **near/far pair** of some kind (a density ramp or two reference distances), though which is still not named by the data or the binary. `.6` is forwarded to the draw command exactly as authored (so it can be negative); `.7` is forwarded after the clamp.
- **`.10` (renderable `.7`, 0 to 2,500) is copied into the draw command unchanged**; nothing in `AddToRendering` reads or transforms it, so its role is still not established.
- **The haze colour's alpha byte is read** (previously open): the draw command's colour field is built by splitting the 32-bit colour into R, G, B *and* alpha bytes (`colour >> 24`); whatever the alpha channel does downstream is in the undecompiled pixel program, but it is not simply discarded.
- The command also carries the un-inset box's own diagonal length (`√(Δx² + Δy² + Δz²)` of the renderable's `Box3f`) as a last field — a single size scalar, not the box itself.

**Worked example** (`1_3_village`'s haze, colour `0x2a383d`, box 300 × 100 × 400, `.5` = 0, `.6` = −150, `.7` = 1,860, `.10` = 0): the fade distance clamps to 0.001, so the camera's intensity scalar is 1.0 until it is within about a millimetre of the box's own surface, then drops to 0.0 — a hard-edged volume. `.7`'s clamp (`max(−150, 0.01, 1860) = 1860`) leaves it unchanged. Contrast `7_1_city_part_i`'s haze (colour `0xa0acbc`, box 3,000 × 2,000 × 3,000, `.5` = 999.8, `.6` = −30, `.7` = 1,300, `.10` = 1,600): here the fade distance is a third of the box's own half-width, so the intensity scalar visibly ramps over hundreds of world units as the camera approaches the boundary from inside, instead of cutting hard.

### Combining several hazes into one screen fog, and its depth basis (T01-412)

T01-070 established that `CHazeEffectRenderable::AddToRendering` (above) never itself calls `shaEnableFog` — it only appends one entry per haze per frame to a shared, growable list. The missing link, traced by this task: that list lives **inside the current `SeriousEngine::CViewRenCmd`** (`Engine.dll`, read in full, **medium**, binary), the per-view render command. `CRenCmd`'s base constructor stores the globally tracked *current view* pointer (set by `CViewRenCmd::ActivateCollection` when a view is activated) into every render command it creates, including the one `CRenderable::AddEffectRenCmd` hands back to `AddToRendering`; and the growable array `AddToRendering` appends into is laid out at the exact same field offsets (capacity, data pointer, count, growth step) as `CViewRenCmd`'s own fields. So every haze's per-frame draw-command entry lands in that frame's active view object, and that same view's own `ActivateExecution` — called from `CViewRenCmd::Execute` immediately before the view's objects are drawn — is what reads the list back and calls `shaEnableFog`.

- **`shaEnableFog` is called at most once per view per frame, from `ActivateExecution`, and only when that view's haze-entry list is non-empty**; an empty list (no haze contributed this frame) skips the call entirely, leaving fog disabled (`Execute`/`DeactivateExecution` bracket every view with `shaDisableFog()`). This is the "what calls `shaEnableFog` with which near/far/colour" answer T01-070 could not reach: **the view itself**, from the same per-frame list `AddToRendering` builds — not a fixed "nearest" or "level-wide default" lookup.
- **The combination is a fade-weighted average over every haze that contributed an entry this frame, not a single winning haze.** `AddToRendering` only appends an entry when that haze's own `fade` (above) is non-zero, so fully-outside hazes never reach this step. For the entries that do, `ActivateExecution` sums every entry's `fade` (`Σfade`) and computes the final near (`.6`) and far (`.7`) as `Σ(fade_i · .6_i) / Σfade` and `Σ(fade_i · .7_i) / Σfade` — a straight fade-weighted average, never a maximum or a single nearest/strongest pick. **When `Σfade < 1`** (no haze, nor their sum, is "fully in" this frame) **the engine additionally scales the averaged near down by `Σfade` and divides the averaged far by `Σfade`**: `near_final = Σfade · near_avg`, `far_final = far_avg / Σfade`. So a lone, partially faded haze does not use its own raw `.6`/`.7` unmodified — the whole range widens towards "no fog" (near → 0, far → ∞, so `scale = 1/(far−near) → 0`) as the total fade weakens, rather than snapping between hazes. The same loop runs four more per-entry numeric channels through an analogous running accumulation, in lock-step with `fade` — these are the haze colour's R, G, B and alpha bytes, in that order, immediately before the `.6`/`.7` fields. **T01-415 (below) pins down, at instruction level, which accumulator is which byte, and corrects two things this task could only guess at from the decompiled C**: the per-entry weight actually used for colour, near and far alike is not plain `fade_i`, and the per-frame maximum box-diagonal this task found unused by `shaEnableFog` turns out to be read elsewhere in this very function after all — see "The exact per-channel arithmetic..." below for both corrections and the clip-plane call they also explain.
- **The combined result also folds into the camera's own clip planes, at low confidence.** When the combined `.10` average is positive and less than the camera's own far clip distance, `ActivateExecution` calls the view's clip-distance setter with the camera's own near clip and (apparently) that value, narrowing the far clip plane. The decompiled C's argument list here is inconsistent (a matrix where a float is expected), a sign of lost parameters; **T01-415 (below) confirms this reading is right and resolves the argument list from the disassembly** — the "matrix" is a struct-return display artefact, not a real fourth parameter or a dropped one.
- **Worked example** (`1_2_approachingvillage`, two simultaneous hazes, `environment-levels.md`: haze A colour `0xff4998bf`, `.6` = −150, `.7` = 20; haze B colour `0xabdcff`, `.6` = 0, `.7` = 5,000): with the camera placed so only A contributes this frame (fade 0.3; B's own fade is 0, so B is never appended), `Σfade = 0.3 < 1`: the one-entry average is just A's own values, then scaled down: `near_final = 0.3 × −150 = −45`, `far_final = 20 / 0.3 ≈ 66.7` — visibly weaker and wider-ranged than A's own authored −150/20, even with only one haze contributing (unaffected by the T01-415 correction below, since a single haze's own box-diagonal trivially equals the frame's maximum). With the camera deep inside both (fade 1.0 each, `Σfade = 2.0 ≥ 1`, no extra scaling): **T01-415 corrects this half of the example** — see "Worked example, corrected" below; the naive equal-weight blend this line previously gave (`near = −75`, `far = 2,510`) is not what the engine computes once the two hazes' very different box sizes are accounted for.
- **The fog's distance basis is depth, not Euclidean view distance** (shipped `Bin/GPU-Programs.asm`, data, not decompiler output, **medium**): the shade family's `Output` vertex-program fragment computes its fog term from the vertex's own post-model-view-projection position (clip-space depth), scaled and biased by the constants `shaGetFogFactors` returns — the same `-1/(far−near)`/`+far/(far−near)`-shaped pair `shaders.md` already names generically for "the fog scale and bias." A separate, unrelated per-material "distance fade" term in the same fragment does use true 3-D Euclidean distance (vertex to the viewer position in object space) to fade translucency near the camera, but that effect does not feed `shaEnableFog` and is not atmospheric fog.
- **Fixed-function fog, not a pixel shader, and not per-haze.** For ordinary opaque surfaces the vertex program writes its depth-based fog factor to the hardware's own per-vertex fog output register — genuine D3D fixed-function fog, armed by `shaGetFogFactors`'s calls into the graphics backend's fog-enable/fog-range/fog-colour state. For alpha-blended (translucent) surfaces, where hardware vertex fog does not composite correctly with blending, the same depth-based term instead darkens the surface's own alpha in the vertex program (the "fog scale and bias for … translucent objects" pair `shaders.md` already names) — still a single value per view per frame, still fed from the one combiner above. **There is no separate per-haze or per-pixel compositing shader**: by the time any material's vertex program runs, every active haze has already been reduced, once per frame on the CPU, to the single colour/near/far triple a program's fog term reads.
- **The sky is probably not fogged, but this is not conclusively settled.** `CCubeRenderable::RenderObject` (the sky cube's own draw call) never calls `shaEnableFog`/`shaDisableFog`/`shaGetFogFactors`; it draws through `gfuUseSimpleShader`, a fixed low-level path that bypasses the whole material/vertex-program selection the fog term above lives in, so the sky's own draw never computes a fog factor. But D3D's fixed-function per-vertex fog, once armed for the frame by an earlier object's `shaGetFogFactors` call, is a render-state that keeps applying to later draws (including a `gfuUseSimpleShader` one) until something disables it again; whether the sky is drawn before any fog-using object this frame (fog still off), after one (fog still on, by render-state leakage), or whether `gfuUseSimpleShader` resets fog itself, was **not traced** by T01-070/T01-412 — see the next section (T01-416).

### The exact per-channel arithmetic, the box-diagonal weight, and the clip-plane call's real arguments, at instruction level (T01-415)

T01-412 could not resolve three details from the decompiled C listing alone, because the x87 floating-point stack bookkeeping past the `fade`/`.6`/`.7` fields is exactly where the decompiler's variable tracking breaks down (the same class of limitation T01-416 separately flags for `Execute`'s own sort comparator). Reading `ActivateExecution`'s raw disassembly together with its companion `CHazeEffectRenderable::AddToRendering`'s (`Engine.dll`, **medium**, binary, both read in full for this task) settles all three:

- **Which accumulator is which colour byte.** `AddToRendering` splits the haze's own packed 32-bit colour (alpha in the top byte, confirming the existing `colour >> 24` alpha finding) into four separate numeric fields it writes into the draw-command entry, in this fixed order: red (`(colour >> 16) & 0xFF`), green (`(colour >> 8) & 0xFF`), blue (`colour & 0xFF`), then alpha (`colour >> 24`) — immediately before the near (`.6`) and far (`.7`) fields. `ActivateExecution`'s combining loop reads these same four fields back in that same order and, after weighting and accumulating each across every contributing haze this frame (see below), rounds and clamps each to a byte and re-packs them into the identical layout — alpha in the top byte, then red, then green, then blue — before handing the packed 32-bit value to `shaEnableFog` as its colour argument. **Red, green and blue are the first three per-entry accumulators, in that order, and alpha is the fourth**; the combined colour keeps the source hazes' own alpha-high byte layout throughout, so alpha genuinely is averaged the same way as the other three channels, not dropped or packed separately.
- **The "apparent off-by-one" is a second, different running sum, not a skipped entry.** Every contributing haze's `fade` is added, unweighted and in full, into the plain total (`Σfade`) that the "widen the range when `Σfade < 1`" rule reads — no entry is skipped there, and T01-412's formula for that specific check is exactly right. But the denominator that red, green, blue, alpha, near and far are actually divided by to form their averages is **not** `Σfade`. Before adding each entry's `fade` into that other running sum, the loop first divides it by *that entry's own* (un-inset) box-diagonal length and multiplies it by **this frame's largest contributing haze's own box-diagonal length** — the very same per-frame maximum `AddToRendering`'s companion pass already computes before this loop runs, which T01-412 could only confirm was computed, not that it was read again. So the true per-entry weight feeding colour, near and far alike is `fade_i × (maxDiagonal / diagonal_i)`, not `fade_i` alone. Because the shared per-frame `maxDiagonal` factor multiplies every term of both the numerator and this denominator identically, it cancels completely out of the final near/far/colour *ratios* — a single contributing haze (whose own diagonal trivially equals the frame's maximum) still averages to exactly its own raw values, leaving T01-412's one-haze worked example unchanged. It does **not** cancel once two or more simultaneous hazes have different box sizes: the smaller-boxed haze's `fade` then counts for proportionally more than an equal `fade` from a larger-boxed one — a genuine size normalisation (a small, deliberately-placed haze outweighs a large ambient one at equal fade), not the plain fade-weighted average T01-412 described at medium-low confidence. The fourth numeric channel (`.10`) and the "third" width-only channel go through the identical weighting. This also directly corrects T01-412's "neither the averaged `.10` nor the maximum diagonal is passed to `shaEnableFog` or read anywhere else in this function": the maximum diagonal **is** read again here, as the common numerator of every entry's weight.
- **The clip-distance call's real arguments.** The decompiled C shows a `Matrix44f`-sized local where a `float` parameter is expected because the callee returns a `Matrix44f` by value through a hidden pointer parameter that the decompiler folds into the visible argument list at the wrong position — a display artefact of the struct-return calling convention, not evidence that the original code passes a matrix where a scalar belongs. The disassembly's actual push order gives the real call: a reference to the current view's own projection matrix (the hidden return buffer aside), the camera's own near clip distance (freshly read off that same matrix), and, as the third and last argument — the position a "far clip" parameter would occupy — the very same per-frame average of the fourth numeric channel (`.10`) the colour/near/far loop above computes, not a separate value. So `.10`'s one and only consumer in this function is this clip-plane call: when its per-frame average is positive and still closer than the camera's own existing far clip plane, `ActivateExecution` narrows that view's far clip plane to it for the frame, exactly as T01-412 already described in prose. Cross-checked against `Core.dll`'s own exported, undecorated signature for the callee, which independently confirms a by-reference matrix parameter followed by two plain floats.
- **Worked example, corrected** (`1_2_approachingvillage`'s two hazes, continuing T01-412's own example, `environment-levels.md`): haze A's box (608.6 × 16.22 × 116.2) has a diagonal of about 619.8 world units; haze B's box (10,000³) has a diagonal of about 17,320.5 — roughly 28 times larger. With the camera placed so only A contributes (fade 0.3, B's fade 0), the result is unchanged from T01-412's own example: `near_final = −45`, `far_final ≈ 66.7` (A's own diagonal trivially equals the frame's maximum, so the extra weighting cancels). With the camera deep inside both (fade 1.0 each): the corrected, diagonal-weighted average gives `near ≈ −144.8` and `far ≈ 192.0` — close to haze A's own raw `.6`/`.7` (−150/20), not the simple 50/50 blend (`−75`/`2,510`) a plain fade-weighted average would give, because A's much smaller box outweighs B's by roughly the same 28:1 ratio as their diagonals. Averaging the two hazes' colours (A `0xff4998bf`, B `0xabdcff` with alpha byte `0x00`) by that same 28:1-favouring-A weight gives the `shaEnableFog` colour argument approximately `0xf64c9ac1` (alpha ≈ 246, red ≈ 76, green ≈ 154, blue ≈ 193) — again close to A's own colour, not an even blend. Confidence **medium** throughout this section (binary, both functions read in full; the cancellation algebra and the worked-example arithmetic themselves are exact, high confidence, once the weight formula is read off the disassembly).

### The sky cube's fog state: the gate is `shaGetFogFactors`, not `shaEnableFog` (T01-416)

T01-412 left two open sub-questions about the sky's fog: whether `gfuUseSimpleShader` resets fog itself, and what the per-frame render-command draw order actually is. Reading `gfuUseSimpleShader` in full (`Engine.dll`, binary, **medium**) answers the first directly: it only binds two tiny, engine-internal vertex/pixel programs (hard-coded source strings compiled once at start-up, not part of the per-material `Shaders.dll`/`GPU-Programs.asm` family) and uploads the model-view-projection matrix as a shader constant — it never calls `shaEnableFog`, `shaDisableFog` or `shaGetFogFactors`, and never touches any fog-related render state. So the sky's own draw neither arms nor disarms fog, confirming the premise, but refining *why* render-state leakage would or would not reach it:

- **`shaEnableFog` (called once per view per frame by `CViewRenCmd::ActivateExecution`, T01-412 above) only updates a *logical* flag and the near/far/colour it will use** — it does not itself touch the GPU's hardware fog-enable bit. That bit is armed (`gfxEnableFog`) or left disarmed (`gfxDisableFog`) only inside `shaGetFogFactors`, which every ordinary *material* draw calls once to fetch its own fog scale/bias constants — reading the logical flag `shaEnableFog` set and flipping the GPU bit to match, every time it runs.
- **`CViewRenCmd::Execute` unconditionally calls `shaDisableFog` (a direct `gfxDisableFog`, not the lazy flag) at the very end of every view**, and again before activating a chained child view. So the hardware fog bit always starts a view's draw loop in the **disarmed** state left by whichever view executed immediately before it — not in whatever state the current view's own (not-yet-executed) haze combination implies.
- Net effect: within one view, the sky is fogged **only if** at least one ordinary material already drew (and so already called `shaGetFogFactors`, arming the bit) earlier in that *same* view's sorted render-command list; if the sky draws before any such material, the hardware bit is still off from the previous view's teardown, regardless of whether this view's own haze combination would enable fog.
- **Whether the sky actually sorts before or after the rest of a view's draws could not be pinned down.** `CViewRenCmd::Execute` sorts its collected render commands with `qsort` before executing them, by a 24-bit field read from each command (plus a secondary tie-break function for equal keys) — very likely a shader/material grouping key, perhaps with a layer tag in unused high bits, but the decompiled C does not expose the comparator's actual numeric return value (the real computation is lost to decompilation, the same class of limit T01-415 already flags for the haze combiner), so neither "background sorts first" nor "background sorts last" could be confirmed this way. The sky's own render command is queued from a second, smaller renderable list `CViewRenCmd::Collect` walks *after* its main scene-renderable list — consistent with, but not proof of, the sky being appended (and so, before sorting, ordered) after the rest of the frame's objects.

**Reader rule (unchanged from before, now for a sharper reason):** treat the sky as **not fogged** — it matches the sky's own draw call, which never arms fog, and is the safer default when the actual sort order is unknown. A follow-up task should resolve the `qsort` comparator and the sky's own sort key at instruction level (T01-422, `tools/re/` and/or Ghidra disassembly, not the decompiled C) before this reader rule can be confirmed or reversed with confidence.

### Whether `shaGetFogFactors` reads the haze colour's alpha byte, and whether it changes the rendered fog (T01-427)

T01-415 confirmed `ActivateExecution` averages the haze colour's alpha byte the same way as red/green/blue and packs it, alpha-high, into the one colour value `shaEnableFog` stores. Reading `SeriousEngine::shaEnableFog` itself (`Engine.dll`, read in full, **medium**, binary) confirms the other half of that link directly, rather than by field-offset matching: its first parameter is stored **unmodified** into the exact same global `shaGetFogFactors` later reads back, alongside the near/far pair and the per-view "fog enabled" flag — the three storage cells this task traces are the very ones `shaEnableFog` writes.

- **Yes, on the opaque path, and the decompiled C actively hides this.** `shaGetFogFactors`'s decompiled C shows the stored colour read, cast to `float`, then cast back to an integer before being handed to the GPU colour setter — reading as a value-changing round trip through a `float` local the function also uses, in other branches, for genuine floating-point fog-scale constants. The raw disassembly (`Engine.dll`, read in full, **medium**, binary) shows this is a decompiler artefact, not real code: there is no floating-point instruction anywhere near this spot, only a plain integer load of the colour value into a register followed by a push of that same register. The full, unmodified 32-bit ARGB value — the same one `shaEnableFog` stored, haze-averaged alpha included — is passed straight through to the GPU fog-colour setter, with no masking, rounding or byte-dropping inside `shaGetFogFactors` itself. This is the function's **opaque** path (its own blending-disabled check).
- **The GPU fog-colour setter forwards it unmodified into Direct3D's own fog-colour render state.** The function pointer resolves into `GfxD3D.dll`'s own small wrapper (read in full, **medium**, binary): it dirty-checks the new colour value against a cached copy and, on a real change, calls Direct3D's render-state setter for the fog colour, passing the colour value through exactly as received. Nothing in this wrapper treats the alpha byte specially, and the dirty-check compares the whole value, so a haze combination that changes only the averaged alpha (RGB unchanged) still triggers a fresh render-state update.
- **Direct3D's own fixed-function fog blend then ignores that alpha byte — public, documented D3D9 behaviour, not something this task needed the binary for:** the fog-colour render state's alpha component is defined by Microsoft's own Direct3D 9 documentation to be ignored by the fog blend (Microsoft Learn, "Fog State (Direct3D 9)" and "Fog Color (Direct3D 9)"; **high** confidence, public documentation). The hardware fog blend — whether driven by the classic fixed-function pipeline or, as `shaders.md`'s `Output` vertex-program fragment does here, by a vertex program writing the legacy per-vertex fog output register — reads only the RGB bytes of that render state.
- **So the answer is the task's third listed option, not the first two:** the haze colour's averaged alpha byte is not fog density/strength and never feeds a blend factor (the translucent-surface scale/bias pair is built solely from the near/far globals, confirmed by the same disassembly — never from the colour global). It *is* the fixed-function fog colour's own alpha channel, read and carried bit-for-bit all the way to the GPU render-state call — and then silently dropped by Direct3D's own fog hardware, which never looks at that channel.
- **Not every material draw even reaches the haze's own colour.** `shaGetFogFactors` additionally branches on the drawn material's own source/destination blend-factor pair (unrelated to which haze is active): for several specific translucent blend-factor combinations it calls the GPU fog-colour setter with a **hardcoded constant** colour instead (alpha byte zero in every one observed) in place of the haze's own value, and for one further specific combination it disables hardware fog outright and never reads the colour global at all, using only the near/far pair to build a scale/bias fed to the vertex program's own alpha-darkening term for that surface. The haze's own averaged colour, alpha included, reaches the GPU fog-colour setter only for ordinary **opaque** draws.
- **Worked example**, continuing T01-415's own `1_2_approachingvillage` example (both hazes contributing, combined colour ≈ `0xf64c9ac1`, alpha ≈ 246): every opaque material drawn in that view has `shaGetFogFactors` hand Direct3D that literal packed value as its fog colour. Direct3D's fixed-function fog blend reads only the RGB bytes out of it (≈ 76, 154, 193); the rendered fog colour would be bit-for-bit identical if the two hazes' alpha channels had averaged to 0 or to 255 instead of 246. The haze colour's own averaged alpha has **no visible effect on the rendered fog** in the original game, despite being faithfully computed, stored and forwarded at every step up to the GPU call.

**Renderer implication: none.** `crates/ss2-render/src/world.wgsl` (`camera.fog_colour`, an RGB colour plus an unrelated engineering "strength" scalar in its alpha slot, never the haze's own stored alpha) and `crates/ss2-world/src/scene.rs`'s `fog_at` (an RGB-only colour field, no alpha at all) already apply linear fog with RGB only — matching the original's own *effective* behaviour exactly, not merely its intent. No change is needed; the haze colour's alpha channel should continue to be ignored by the renderer's fog path.

### `CViewRenCmd::Execute`'s `qsort`: the real comparator, the sort key, and where the sky and bloom get theirs, at instruction level (T01-422)

T01-416 could not read the comparator's real numeric return from the decompiled C: the decompiler shows it returning nothing, and on a tie it hands off to a second function that itself appears to take no visible argument — the same x87-stack-tracking failure T01-415 already flagged for the haze combiner. Reading both functions' raw instruction listings (`Engine.dll`, **medium**, binary, both read in full for this task), then tracing every renderable that writes the field the comparator reads, settles the comparator, the key, and both of this task's worked cases.

- **The comparator subtracts a 24-bit key, then breaks an exact tie with a float field — the second function is a generic rounding helper, not bespoke tie-break logic.** Each render command's own 24-bit sort key is masked out of a shared 32-bit word independently on each side, then subtracted; if the two differ, that signed difference is itself the comparator's result, and because the sort runs ascending, the command with the smaller key always executes first. Only when both keys are bit-for-bit equal does the comparator load a second, per-command floating-point field from each side, subtract them, and hand that difference to the second function. That callee turns out not to be anything specific to rendering: tracing its other callers across the module finds it invoked from dozens of unrelated routines (sockets, fonts, gamepad input, a telnet session, light sources) — it is a generic, compiler-emitted "round a float to the nearest integer" helper, exactly matching the decompiler's own reading of it as a rounding cast. So the real rule is: **sort ascending by the 24-bit key; within an exact tie, sort ascending by a second per-command float field**, with the rounding itself contributed by shared runtime code, not by anything the renderer designed for sorting.
- **That key is a small, hand-picked set of category bits, not a material/shader ID or a layer counter.** Every `AddToRendering` override this task read that sets it — `CModelRenderable`; `CPrimitiveRenderable`/`CPieSliceRenderable`/`CEditorPrimitiveRenderable`, all three identical in shape; the single, linker-folded `CBillboardRenderable`/`CCubeRenderable` pair (below); the debug-only `CVisBoxRenderable`; `CFlareRenderable`; `CHazeEffectRenderable`; and T01-423's `CBloomRenderable` in `Bin/ProcRender.dll` — writes one of a handful of small constants, never a continuously varying value. One particular bit is consistently the "sort this one back-to-front, like a translucent surface" flag: every renderable that sets it also negates its own per-command distance field in the same breath, which — because ties break by *ascending* that field — turns ascending distance into descending distance (farthest-first) for exactly the commands flagged this way, the standard order for correct alpha blending; commands without that bit keep their natural nearest-first distance order, matching early-depth-reject opaque geometry. `CModelRenderable` only sets it once a model's own distance has reached its LOD fade-out threshold — a model moves into the back-to-front group only while it is dithering out at range, not merely for having a blended material. A handful of higher bits of the same word (for example one `CObjRenCmd::Execute` reads as an "occlusion query already in flight" flag, set alongside the key by `CModelRenderable::AddToRendering` under an occlusion-culling render state) are ordinary per-command runtime flags that the comparator's own masking step excludes from the comparison entirely — not sort data.
- **Settling T01-416: the sky's own command carries the same small key as ordinary near/opaque geometry, not the back-to-front one.** `CCubeRenderable::AddToRendering` is not its own function: the linker has folded it, instruction-for-instruction, into the same machine code as `CBillboardRenderable::AddToRendering` (confirmed directly — both mangled names are attached to the one address in the binary's own symbol table), which unconditionally tags its command with the near/opaque key and never negates its distance field. So the sky never competes, on the main key, with the back-to-front/fading bucket a dithering model or an explicitly translucent primitive uses; it sorts alongside ordinary opaque geometry and loses ties the same nearest-first way. This settles T01-416's open question on the main key: **nothing routes the sky into the same group as alpha-blended draws.** Within that shared group, the tie-break is ascending distance from the camera to the *renderable's own stored placement*, run through a point-to-oriented-box test against its bounding box — **not** the camera-recentred position `CCubeRenderable::RenderObject` actually draws with (T01-409: the sky's own placement position is a per-level, largely arbitrary value the draw itself ignores). This task did not re-measure that box-distance across the install, so it cannot say with certainty that the sky always sorts at the very front of its group — only that the *mechanism* is now fully resolved: whichever near/opaque-group command has the smaller camera distance executes (and, if it is an ordinary material, arms the hardware fog bit via `shaGetFogFactors`) before the sky does; nothing in the back-to-front/fading group, and nothing from a different view, can reach the sky this way.
- **Settling part of T01-423's open question: haze and bloom commands sort in this same array, and two bloom commands tie exactly.** Haze and bloom both use `CEffectRenCmd`, a sibling of `CObjRenCmd` under the same base class the comparator reads, appended into the identical per-view growable array `CViewRenCmd::Execute` sorts — so their relative order against each other, and against ordinary object draws, is this same comparator, not a separate effects-only pass. The shared `CEffectRenCmd::Prepare` step always sets the tie-break distance field to exactly **0.0**, for every haze and every bloom, every frame — never a real distance. `CHazeEffectRenderable::AddToRendering` tags its own command with one small, fixed key. **`CBloomRenderable::AddToRendering` (`Bin/ProcRender.dll`) tags every bloom command with a single, different, equally fixed key, unconditionally** — not derived from the entity's box, position, camera distance, or anything else per-entity. So **two simultaneous bloom entities' commands are bit-for-bit tied on the main key and tied again on the distance tie-break (zero against zero)**: the comparator returns exactly zero for every pair of bloom commands in the same frame, regardless of which two bloom entities they are. A C `qsort` is not required to leave equal elements in their original relative order, so nothing in `CViewRenCmd::Execute` *guarantees* which of two tied bloom commands runs first — and, just as importantly, nothing in the comparator or either renderable's own `AddToRendering` makes that choice by entity order, box size or camera distance either: those fields simply never reach the comparison. Whatever order two tied bloom commands end up executing in is an accident of the sort's own handling of equal keys combined with the order `CViewRenCmd::Collect` visited the two entities while walking the view's scene list before the sort ran (the level's own object-list order) — not a rule the original engine designed.
  - **Worked example (`dm_hole`):** "Bloom_PoolRoom" and "Bloom001" (T01-423's own worked example) tie exactly on both comparator fields; there is no box-size or distance rule to apply here — whichever bloom entity the level's own object list visits first is, in practice, the one `efxBloom` runs (and composites) first, and the other sees its already-brightened result.

**Reader rule, bloom order:** there is no original sort rule to match for two simultaneous bloom entities — the comparator ties exactly, by construction, on every field it reads. `LevelScene::blooms_at`'s existing "list order" behaviour (`crates/ss2-world/src/scene.rs`) already matches what actually happens in the original (the order `CViewRenCmd::Collect`'s own list walk visits the entities, since nothing downstream reorders an exact tie by anything the entities themselves carry); its doc comment can now drop "its exact order is open, T01-422" as a settled question rather than a gap. No renderer change is needed.

**Reader rule, sky/fog (refines reader invariant 10):** keep treating the sky as not fogged. The mechanism is now fully resolved: the sky's command never falls into the back-to-front/fading group that would place it among alpha-blended draws; it competes only against ordinary near/opaque-group commands by ascending camera distance (not negated). Only another near/opaque-group command whose own camera distance is smaller than the sky's can execute — and so arm `shaGetFogFactors` — before the sky does; the sky's own distance for this purpose is a point-to-box test against its largely arbitrary placement position (T01-409), not the camera-recentred position it is actually drawn at, so this task cannot say the sky invariably wins that tie-break across the whole install — a narrow, genuine gap left for a camera-position sweep of the shipped `.wld` placements, not more static reading. **T01-428 (below) ran that sweep: the sky loses this tie-break almost everywhere, which reverses this reader rule.**

### Measuring the sky's tie-break distance against real camera positions: it reliably loses (T01-428)

T01-422 settled the mechanism but, reading the binary alone, could not say whether the sky's own point-to-box distance against its stored placement (T01-409: "non-zero, seemingly arbitrary," with "no relation to the level's playable area") is usually small enough to win its tie-break against nearby ordinary opaque draws, whose own distance is the real camera distance. This task answers that with a data script over the retail install (`tools/re/sky_sort_distance_verify.py`, read-only, **high** confidence for the counts) instead of more Ghidra work, exactly as the roadmap task proposed.

For every one of the 58 sky worlds, the script computes the sky's own point-to-box distance — the same clamped-per-axis-then-measure construction already used for the haze/bloom camera-distance test elsewhere in this file, against a box centred and oriented by the sky's own `en_qvPlacement` with half-extent `en_fSize` — from every representative camera position it can find for that level: the single-player/co-op start (the world's first chapter in the chapter chain, the same entity `player_start_verify.py`/`chapter_start_ground_verify.py` already use for this purpose) and, for deathmatch levels, every `CSpawnMarkerEntity`. It compares that against the distance from the same camera position to the *nearest* `CStaticModelEntity`'s own placement — a lower-bound proxy for how close some ordinary opaque command's own (real, not negated) camera distance already is at that position, since retail levels are built from tiled static models and `chapter_start_ground_verify.py` already found one routinely within a 15 m radius of a chapter start.

- **The sky's own distance is almost always enormous; the nearest static model's is almost always tiny.** Across 227 camera samples over all 58 skies: the sky's own point-to-box distance ranges from 11.3 to 53,200.4 world units (median 1,316.7), while the nearest static model's own placement distance ranges from 0.3 to 53.0 (median 5.6) — two to five orders of magnitude apart in the typical case. This is the direct, measured consequence of T01-409's own finding that the sky's placement position has no relation to the level's playable area: the point-to-box test measures distance to that unrelated position (inset only by the small, almost always 64-unit, half-extent), not to anything near the camera.
- **The nearest static model alone already beats the sky in 226 of 227 sampled camera positions, across all 58 of the 58 sky worlds.** Every single-player/co-op start and every deathmatch spawn marker this script could resolve has at least one `CStaticModelEntity` closer to the camera than the sky's own tie-break distance — so that model's render command sorts, and (being an ordinary opaque material) arms `shaGetFogFactors`, strictly before the sky's command does. This is the outcome at essentially every sampled camera position in the data set, not a property of a handful of unusual worlds.
- **The sole exception (`dm_yodeller`, one of its 9 spawn/start cameras) is a near-miss, not a reversal.** `dm_yodeller`'s sky has, by far, the smallest measured placement-to-camera distance in the data set (11.3–17.2 across its 9 cameras, against medians in the thousands elsewhere), because its sky's arbitrary placement happens to land close to the map; even so, the nearest static model still beats the sky's distance at 8 of those 9 cameras, and the one exception (sky 12.8, nearest static model 14.1) is a margin of 1.3 world units against a single proxy model, not against every opaque command actually in view — the closest the whole install ever comes to the sky winning, not a case where it clearly does.
- **Worked examples at the extremes:** `6_3_snow_base`'s single-player start measures 53,200.4 world units from its sky's placement box (nearest static model: 5.6) — any wall, floor or prop the player can see there is nearer than that. `dm_chanolin`'s 16 spawn markers measure 37,291.3–37,300.5 (nearest static models: 2.2–8.1), consistent with T01-409's own `-37,346` Y-axis example for this same world. At the install's smallest extreme, `dm_landofgiants` still measures 93.3–125.5 against static models at 3.8–15.0.

**Renderer consequence: the "sky never fogged" default was the safe guess, not the measured one — it is not safe across the install.** T01-412/T01-416 already established, at medium confidence, that D3D's fixed-function fog render state, once armed by an ordinary material's `shaGetFogFactors` call, keeps applying to later draws in the same view (including the sky's own `gfuUseSimpleShader` one) until something disables it again, and that the sky's own draw neither arms nor disarms it. This task's measurement closes the remaining gap T01-422 left open: because the sky's own tie-break distance is, in every one of the 58 sky worlds, reliably far larger than genuinely nearby opaque geometry's real camera distance, an ordinary opaque command almost always executes — and so arms the hardware fog bit — before the sky's own command does, in the same view. So whenever that view's own haze combination is non-empty on a given frame (reader invariants 8/9: at least one haze's `fade` is non-zero), the original effectively **does** render the sky with hardware fog already armed, not disarmed — the opposite of the previous default. This holds for all 58 sky worlds measured here, not a named subset of them: the "sky draws before any fogged material" case the earlier sections treated as the uncertain possibility is, by this measurement, the rare one (1 sampled camera out of 227, and even there only against a single nearby model, not a guarantee against every opaque command in view). **Still open:** whether the sky's own `gfuUseSimpleShader` vertex program writes a value that makes the already-armed fog visible on its own draw, as opposed to the render state merely being armed and unused by that particular fixed low-level path — this task measured the sort order and the render-state mechanism, not that program's own output (follow-up task, end of this file).

### The bloom pipeline's shape, and why its blend formula stays open (T01-070)

**`Shaders.dll` is now decompiled (T01-412), and the name search still comes up empty.** Of its roughly 1,685 functions, 1,482 are nameless (`FUN_…`); the ~200 named ones are exactly the material-shader classes and their argument structs already known from `mtr.md`/`shaders.md` (`CShadeShader`, `CWaterShader`, `CReflectionShader`, …) — no class or string anywhere in the module is named `*Bloom*` or `*Haze*`. ### `CBloomRenderable`'s own field layout, confirmed field-by-field (T01-417, binary, medium)

`CBloomRenderable` is never referenced by name anywhere in `Engine.dll` (confirmed again, the same exhaustive check as before, now also across every one of its roughly 15,500 indexed functions, not only the named ones). It *is* referenced, by name, in exactly one place in the whole decompiled project: a `Sam2Game.dll` routine — not previously located by T01-070/T01-412's name searches, because it is itself unnamed and only calls into the (separately named, because they are imported/RTTI-resolved) `CBloomRenderable` constructor, destructor and `operator>>` — that rebuilds both a `CBloomEntity`'s editor-only placement gizmo and its live `CBloomRenderable` straight from the `CBloomProperties` record on every property edit. Reading it in full gives a field-by-field confirmation of the "the renderable copies the record" claim above, plus several clamp ranges not visible from the data alone:

- The record's colour copies straight across to renderable `.3`, unclamped — confirming the existing colour mapping directly rather than by value-range matching.
- The record's three box-size floats (already suspected, not checked, as a box) become renderable `.1` (a `Box3f`): the routine scales each by a pair of constants that this task did not itself read numerically, but that the same module uses elsewhere, always with the same opposite signs, as a generic "centre ± half-extent" idiom for building other entities' boxes. The consistent reuse makes a **centred** box (half of each record size on either side of the entity's own placement) the best-supported reading, not an offset one.
- Immediately after the box, one record float is clamped to zero with **no** upper bound before being copied to renderable `.2` — the same shape (floor-clamped, uncapped, same position right after the box) as haze's own `.5` edge-fade member (T01-070).
- The next record float (the **first** of the record's two same-named `.21` members, T01-424) is clamped to `[0, 4]` before being copied, to renderable `.19` — and in the retail data is **always authored as exactly 3** (34/34 instances, `tools/re/wld_bloom_members_verify.py`), matching the existing "remaining constants (1.0, 0, 3)" note.
- The following record float (`.16`) is clamped to `[0, 1]` before being copied, to renderable `.13`.
- The next record float (`.6`) is clamped to `[0, 2]` before being copied, to renderable `.4` (0.3–1.535 observed, T01-424: this is the **strength multiplier**, below — not a threshold, despite the small `.4` label).
- The record's **second** `.21` member is likewise clamped to `[0, 2]` before being copied, to renderable `.18` (0.5, 0.846, 1.5 or 2 observed, 4 distinct across all 34 instances — the **colour-ramp selector**, below).
- The next record float (`.8`) is clamped to `[0, 8]` before being copied, to renderable `.6` — `.6`'s 0.5–6 range is the widest of the numeric members, matching a clamp this loose.
- The record's `.13` member is clamped to `[0.1, 10]` before being copied, to renderable `.12` (0.657, 1 or 1.5 observed, 3 distinct — a **blur two-pass balance factor**, below, settling T01-423's open "`.12`/`.18`" question together with the previous two bullets: T01-424).

- Four more record fields (`.10`, `.11`, `.18`, `.19`) copy straight through with no clamp at all to renderable `.8`, `.9`, `.15`, `.16`, each always authored 1.0 or 0 in the retail data; a final record field (`.20`) is clamped `[0, 1]` and copied to renderable `.17`, always authored 1.0.

### Which renderable member feeds which `BloomEffect` stage (T01-417, corrected and completed by T01-424)

T01-417 cross-referenced `GPU-Programs.asm`'s own register documentation for the `BloomEffect` programs against value ranges alone (medium-low confidence throughout, since it did not yet have either class's own CTSEMETA member list). **T01-424 read the actual CPU-side `efxBloom` free function in `Bin/ProcRender.dll` in full** (binary, medium confidence) and separately confirmed every member's real number from the classes' own type tables stored in the `.wld` data itself (data, high confidence, `tools/re/wld_bloom_members_verify.py`), which corrects two of T01-417's role assignments and settles the two it left open:

- **Downscale**: `efxBloom` runs the family's box filter in a loop, ping-ponging between two small off-screen render targets, **quartering both dimensions each iteration** (`new = ceil(old / 4)`) **until both are ≤ 128 texels** — confirming the existing "4×4 downscale" finding and adding the concrete stopping size and iteration count (2 iterations from a 1080p-class back buffer, 3 from a 4K one; never a single fixed pass). No `CBloomRenderable` member is read anywhere in this loop's own control flow — confirming "no per-entity constant" directly from the code, not by elimination.
- **High-pass / contrast**: fused into the *same* downscale loop, not a separate pass. Each iteration also draws two offset copies of the current downscaled image, additively blended (a cheap 2-tap running blur, see "Blur" below) — so the family's dedicated high-pass pixel program runs once, **after** the ping-pong loop exits, on the now ≤128-texel result. It reads renderable `.13` (fed by record `.16`, clamp `[0, 1]`), scaled down by the square root of the per-call intensity (camera fade × strength × `efx_fBloomBias`, below) and clamped to ≤1, as a uniform "how much of the high-pass result to keep" amount — **not** a mask-blend factor (T01-417's guess for this slot's role; the number assignment itself, `.13` fed by `.16`, was already correct). Separately, **only when HDR rendering is active**, renderable `.19` (fed by the record's *first* `.21`, clamp `[0, 4]`, always authored exactly 3) is subtracted from a fixed headroom constant to bias the pass further; **under standard (non-HDR) rendering this member is never read at all** and the threshold is effectively a hard-coded 0 — so in the common case this per-entity "threshold" has **no effect whatsoever**, a genuinely new finding (T01-424, binary, medium).
- **Blur**: not a separate dedicated pass either — it is the pair of offset-and-additively-blend draws inside the SAME downscale loop (one pass per axis, each contributing two draws at `+offset`/`-offset`), plus one more such pair right after the loop. The per-axis texel offset is renderable `.6` (fed by record `.8`, clamp `[0, 8]`, the widest numeric member) scaled by a fixed fraction of the working texture's texel size — **confirming `.6` as the blur spread, as T01-417 already guessed**, and additionally pinning the fixed per-tap offset itself at **1.5 texels** (the classic two-tap bilinear trick for a wider effective kernel; binary, medium — this exact number lives in `ProcRender.dll`'s own data, not in `GPU-Programs.asm`'s program text, so it is reported here rather than only described by role). Renderable `.12` (fed by record `.13`, clamp `[0.1, 10]`, values 0.657/1/1.5 across the install) splits the blur strength **asymmetrically between the two offset-pair draws** (one draw's offset is multiplied by `.12`, or divided by it, depending on whether `.12` is above or below 1) — a **blur balance/anisotropy factor**, not a mask-blend or a strength/ramp role (T01-424, binary, medium; this settles T01-417's open "`.12`/`.18`" ambiguity for `.12`'s own identity, separately from T01-423/T01-424's strength/ramp question below, which turns out to be about `.4`/`.18` instead).
- **Add / composite**: the final draw reads the entity's own colour (`.3`), unpacks its RGB channels, each **multiplied by the per-call camera fade (not the full intensity) and clamped to ≤1**, then scaled by a further factor that is 1 under standard rendering (and a fixed constant under the alternate code path exercised only when a mask texture is bound — never, in retail, since `.7` is null in all 34 instances). **The colour's alpha channel is read and used unscaled by the fade** — bypassing the per-frame camera gate entirely, a previously-unconfirmed detail (binary, medium). The blend mode is a standard alpha-weighted additive blend; a module-global flag selects an alternate (fully additive, ignoring alpha) mode instead. A mask texture, if bound, modulates the composite through a second texture unit with a from-scratch UV-scroll animation driven by two more renderable constants (`.8`/`.9`, fed by record `.10`/`.11`, always 1.0) — entirely **dead code in the retail game**, since no instance ships a mask; the fallback is a flat gray texture, matching the existing "mask is inert" finding but now attached to the correct member (record `.20` → renderable `.17`, not `.13`).
- **The three-anchor colour ramp**: a *third*, separate pixel pass, bound and run after the downscale/high-pass/blur loop and before the final additive composite, reads renderable `.18` (fed by the record's *second* `.21`, clamp `[0, 2]`, 4 distinct values across the install: 0.5, 0.846, 1.5, 2) and piecewise-linearly interpolates across three fixed, 4-component colour anchors baked into `ProcRender.dll`'s own data section (not level data, not GPU program text; binary, medium): approximately `(0.66, 0.10, 0.05, 0.02)` at selector 0, `(0.24, 0.21, 0.10, 0.07)` at selector 1 (the shared midpoint both segments blend towards/from), and `(0.16, 0.15, 0.14, 0.13)` at selector 2 — a warm, reddish-orange tint fading through a dimmer neutral tone to a near-grey, low-saturation tone as the selector rises (component order — RGBA, BGRA or similar — not independently confirmed). This settles T01-423's open "colour-ramp selector" role directly: **it is renderable `.18`**, not `.12`.

### The per-frame bloom pipeline: where it lives, and the camera gate (T01-423)

`CBloomRenderable`'s own virtual overrides were never going to show up in `Engine.dll`, `GfxD3D.dll`, `Sam2Game.dll` or `Shaders.dll`: **the class's own code lives in a fifth module, `Bin/ProcRender.dll`** (a 340 KB DLL, not previously decompiled by this project) — confirmed directly (binary, medium): `Sam2Game.dll`'s own import table resolves the `CBloomRenderable` constructor/destructor T01-417 found to `PROCRENDER.DLL`, not `Engine.dll`. Decompiling that one small module finds every one of `CBloomRenderable`'s virtual overrides by name (`AddToRendering`, `RenderEffect`, `GetBoundingBox`, `CheckRay`) plus the free function that actually runs the bloom chain, `SeriousEngine::efxBloom`. This also explains why T01-417 could not find a single caller of `CRenderable::AddEffectRenCmd` for bloom even though bloom calls it just as haze does: the call crosses a DLL boundary a same-module reference search inside `Engine.dll` cannot see.

**`CBloomRenderable::AddToRendering`** (binary, medium) is much shorter than haze's: gated by its own global render-state bit (the "Bloom" video option, a different bit of the same flags word haze's own switch uses, T01-070), it does nothing but call the shared `CRenderable::AddEffectRenCmd` and tag the resulting command as a bloom effect. **It computes no camera distance or fade itself** — unlike `CHazeEffectRenderable::AddToRendering`, which computes its fade inline before appending to the per-view haze list (T01-070/T01-412).

**The shared, non-virtual `CEffectRenCmd::Prepare` step inside `AddEffectRenCmd`** (`Engine.dll`, read in full for this task, binary, medium) is itself a camera-in-box gate, used by *any* `CRenderable` that calls `AddEffectRenCmd` — haze included: it calls the renderable's own virtual `GetBoundingBox()` (the **un-inset** `Box3f`, `.1`), transforms the viewer position into that box's own local, rotated frame using the renderable's placement (the same "camera position in the box's own local frame" idiom as haze's edge-fade test, T01-070), and tests whether the transformed viewer lies strictly inside the box's six faces. If it does not, the command is cancelled right there and nothing further happens this frame for that entity — a coarse, binary pre-filter shared by both effect types.

**`CBloomRenderable::RenderEffect`** (binary, medium) is called once per surviving command when the view executes its collected effect commands (the base `CEffectRenCmd::Execute` dispatches to the renderable's own virtual `RenderEffect` — the same pair haze's own command uses, except haze's own `RenderEffect` override is a no-op, folded together with `CRenderable`'s own empty base implementation: haze never needs it, having already done its camera work in `AddToRendering`). This is where bloom's own edge-fade computation happens, **deferred from add-time to render-time**: it re-transforms the viewer into the box's own local frame, insets the box per axis by the edge-fade distance (`.2`, floor-clamped to a minimum of 0.001, with the same "axis too thin for the fade — collapse to its own midpoint" rule haze uses), computes the camera's clamped per-axis distance to that inset box, and sets **`fade = 1 − clamp(distance / .2, 0, 1)`** — byte-for-byte the same formula T01-070 documented for haze's `.5`. **This settles part (1) of this task directly, not by structural analogy**: bloom *is* gated by the camera's position inside the entity's box, with an edge fade exactly like haze (medium confidence, binary — the formula is read, not inferred from field ranges).

`RenderEffect` passes that `fade` to **`SeriousEngine::efxBloom`** (a free function, not a method), the per-frame code this task set out to find: it performs the actual screen-space bloom chain — downscale, high-pass, a two-pass separable blur, and an additive composite — directly against whatever is the *current* draw port/back buffer at the moment it is called. A module-global setting, `efx_fBloomBias` (clamped to [0, 10], doubled again under HDR), independently scales every bloom entity's strength for the whole frame — a video-option-level control, not a per-entity one. **T01-424 read `efxBloom` in full** (binary, medium): the per-call intensity is `clamp(fade × renderable.4 × efx_fBloomBias, 0, 2)` (renderable `.4` is the **strength multiplier**, below); below a small fixed intensity floor, or when the current draw port is smaller than 32×32, the whole call is skipped outright (an early-out this task had not previously documented).

### Combining two bloom entities (T01-423)

**Not a fade-weighted average, and not a single pick — bloom has no shared per-frame combiner at all.** Unlike haze's `CViewRenCmd::ActivateExecution` (T01-412), which gathers every contributing haze into one list and blends them, `efxBloom` only ever sees one `CBloomRenderable` and one `fade` value per call. Each `CBloomEntity` gets its own independent `CEffectRenCmd`, independently gated by `Prepare`'s box cull and independently faded by `RenderEffect`; when two bloom entities both pass these gates in the same frame, their two `RenderEffect`/`efxBloom` calls simply run one after the other against the *same* draw port. Because `efxBloom` reads and processes whatever is already in that draw port at the moment it runs, **the second call's own downscale/high-pass stage sees a scene that already includes the first call's additive bloom glow** — the two contributions do not sum as independent, order-independent quantities; the later call can re-brighten the earlier one's own result, and which one runs first (decided by the view's own sorted render-command order, `CViewRenCmd::Execute`'s `qsort`) changes the outcome. **T01-422 (below) settles that order at instruction level: two bloom commands always tie exactly on every field the comparator reads**, so the original has no deliberate rule here either — a renderer that wants to match this exactly should still execute bloom entities one at a time, each against the real current frame buffer, but in the level's own object-list order (what the sort leaves an exact tie as), not a combined set of constants up front the way the haze fog combiner uses.

A directly-read side effect, not asked for by this task but found while reading `RenderEffect`: it only does any of this work when the command's own view has **no parent view** (a check against the view-chain pointer every render command's base class stores, T01-412) — so a nested/chained view (a mirror or portal, if any exist in the shipped content) never gets bloom applied to it, regardless of how many bloom entities are active; this is unrelated to entity count and applies identically whether there are zero, one or two bloom entities live.

**Worked example** (`dm_hole`, the one world with two simultaneous `CBloomEntity`s; values from `tools/re/wld_bloom_members_verify.py`, data, high confidence for the record values, binary/medium for which renderable role each feeds): bloom A ("Bloom_PoolRoom", colour `0xff150701`, box 96.2×88.1×123.3 at world position (−65.3, 35.8, −33.1), edge-fade `.2` = 10, strength `.4` = 1.0, ramp selector `.18` = 2.0, blur spread `.6` = 1.205, blur balance `.12` = 1.0, high-pass amount `.13` = 0.5, threshold `.19` = 3 (inert, non-HDR)) and bloom B ("Bloom001", colour `0xff272727`, box 250×250×250 at (−4.1, 13.8, 1.6), edge-fade `.2` = 10, strength `.4` = 1.0, ramp selector `.18` = 1.5, blur spread `.6` = 1.0, blur balance `.12` = 1.0, high-pass amount `.13` = 0.5, threshold `.19` = 3) place their **outer boxes overlapping on every axis, and their fade-inset inner boxes too** (`tools/re/wld_bloom_cameratest_verify.py`, data, high confidence for the positions/boxes/overlap, read from each renderable's own world placement, `CAspect.7`): there is a real region of the map where a camera sees `fade = 1.0` for *both* entities in the same frame. A renderer slice reproducing this level must run both entities' bloom passes, in the engine's own per-view sort order, each reading back the result of whichever ran first, rather than averaging or picking one. (Earlier drafts of this section swapped bloom A/B's `.6` values and used the since-corrected `.12`/`.18` labelling below; this worked example supersedes them, T01-424.)

**Second worked example** (`1_3_Village`, one `CBloomEntity`): colour `0xff463c26`, box 400×148×400 at world position (200.9, 47.3, −192.6), edge-fade `.2` = 25, strength `.4` = 1.2422 (the highest in the install's 17-distinct-value range of 0.3–1.535), ramp selector `.18` = 1.5, blur spread `.6` = 1.0, blur balance `.12` = 1.0, high-pass amount `.13` = 0.15, threshold `.19` = 3. With a camera inside this box's fade-inset core, the per-call intensity is `1.0 × 1.2422 × efx_fBloomBias` (clamped to [0, 2]) — noticeably stronger than either `dm_hole` entity (both strength `.4` = 1.0) for the same camera fade and bias.

### Which `BloomEffect` role feeds the strength multiplier and which feeds the colour-ramp selector (T01-423, settled by T01-424)

T01-423 found that two of `CBloomRenderable`'s members are read directly by `efxBloom` in two distinct roles — a per-entity strength multiplier, and a selector into a small, fixed, three-anchor colour ramp built into `ProcRender.dll` itself (not read from any entity or world data; see "Which renderable member feeds which `BloomEffect` stage" above for the ramp's own values) — but could not tell which renderable number (`.12` or `.18`) was which, because `CBloomProperties`' own CTSEMETA member-offset table had not been located.

**T01-424 locates that table directly, as game data, not as a binary structure.** `CBloomProperties` (like every stored CTSEMETA class) declares its own member list in the type table of every `.wld` that places one, in file-declaration order (`docs/re/ctsemeta-types.md`'s `STMB` block) — the same order the `Sam2Game.dll` copy routine reads them in. `CBloomRenderable` declares its own member list the same way (it is serialized into the world's object table too, alongside the entity's record, for the editor's own convenience — not purely a runtime-only construction as earlier tasks assumed). Reading both tables by position, not by name (`tools/re/wld_bloom_members_verify.py`, data, high confidence, 34/34 instances in all 33 worlds):

- **The strength multiplier is renderable `.4`** (fed by record `.6`), not `.12`. This is the member `efxBloom` multiplies by the per-frame camera `fade` and `efx_fBloomBias` (above); its value range (0.3–1.535, 17 distinct) is exactly the range T01-417 had — reasonably, from value ranges alone — attributed to a high-pass *threshold* instead, under the mistaken assumption that the renderable's own number for that slot was also `.4`. It is not: the clamp-`[0,4]`-and-always-3 slot (the one T01-417's reasoning actually describes) is a *different* member, renderable `.19`.
- **The colour-ramp selector is renderable `.18`** (fed by the record's *second* `.21`, i.e. `CBloomProperties.21`'s second occurrence in its own type table), not `.12`. Its value range (0.5, 0.846, 1.5, 2 — 4 distinct across the install) matches the ramp's own two-segment domain `[0, 2]` exactly.
- **Renderable `.12`** (fed by record `.13`, i.e. `CBloomProperties.13`) is neither role: it is the **blur pass's own balance/anisotropy factor** between the two offset-blend draws each downscale iteration performs ("Which renderable member feeds which `BloomEffect` stage" above) — a third, previously unidentified role, settling why T01-417/T01-423's reasoning from value ranges alone (0.657/1/1.5 "landing on the ramp's own middle breakpoint") could not cleanly separate it from the ramp selector: it never fed the ramp at all.

**Answering this task's own question directly: `CBloomProperties.6` is the strength multiplier's record-side source (not `.13` or `.21`); the colour-ramp selector's record-side source is `CBloomProperties.21` — specifically its *second* declared occurrence (the type table genuinely declares a member named "21" twice, confirmed data, high confidence, above). `CBloomProperties.13` feeds neither of the two roles this task was asked to settle; it feeds the blur balance factor instead.** Confidence: **high** for every member's own number and value range (data, read directly from the type tables and object data of all 33 worlds with a `CBloomEntity`, not inferred); **medium** for each number's functional role (binary, `efxBloom` and the `Sam2Game.dll` copy routine read in full, not range-guessed).

### The off-screen canvases' size and pixel-format policy, and the colour ramp's own CPU/GPU split (T01-425)

T01-424 could not say what literal pixel format or size policy `efxBloom`'s off-screen canvases use, because that is decided by a helper this task's predecessor had not yet read, and because the actual backend texture creation crosses into a *sixth* module. Reading that helper (`Bin/ProcRender.dll`, the function `efxBloom` itself calls to get its ping-pong canvases) and following its own canvas-creation call into `Bin/GfxD3D.dll` (binary, medium, both already-decompiled modules; **not** `GfxD3D.dll`'s `gfxGetCanvasDrawPort`/`gfxGetTextureCanvasHandle` as this task's own line guessed — those two names resolve to `Engine.dll`, and are merely accessors for a canvas `efxBloom` already holds, not where one gets created) settles both questions, with a correction to each:

- **Size policy — corrected.** The chain's first canvas size is **not** the quartering loop's own first step, as the renderer's `step_sizes` assumes. Before any quartering, the helper rounds the current draw port's width and height **each independently up to the next power of two**, then **clamps each to [128, 2048]** — only *that* rounded-and-clamped size is what the quarter-each-step (`ceil(side / 4)`, floor 128) loop actually starts from. A renderer matching this exactly must add the power-of-two round-up and the 2048 ceiling before quartering, not quarter the real frame size directly: for a 1920×1080 frame this makes no visible difference to the *step count* (both round up to 2048 either way, then quarter to 512 then 128), but it does change the result for a frame whose real size is not already a power of two near a quartering boundary, and it caps the very first step's size at 2048 regardless of a larger real resolution. The whole canvas chain (plus, under HDR, one extra full-resolution canvas, next point) is **cached across frames**, keyed on this rounded size and on the HDR/non-HDR format class, and only torn down and recreated when either changes — not rebuilt every frame.
- **Pixel-format policy — corrected.** There is no single fixed canvas format. **Under standard (non-HDR) rendering** — the common case — the main downscale/blur chain's canvases use a **16-bit-per-pixel** colour format; only the terminal 128×128 ping-pong pair steps up to a **32-bit-per-pixel** format. **Under HDR rendering**, an *extra* full-resolution canvas exists (used only for the HDR path's own first pass, absent entirely under standard rendering) and the whole chain, including the terminal pair, instead uses a **64-bit-per-pixel** format throughout (consistent with four 16-bit-per-channel components) — confirmed from the engine's own byte-size table for these format codes, read directly (binary, medium), not inferred from names. None of these byte-width classes names one fixed, literal surface format: the concrete backend format is **renegotiated once per GPU/driver at graphics-API start-up**, trying one preferred format and falling back through up to five alternatives per class via Direct3D's own device-capability check — so no single format name (e.g. a specific 16-bit layout) can be reported as *the* canvas format; what is fixed and confirmed is the byte-width class per canvas role, and that the 64-bit class is HDR-only. **The renderer's current blanket choice of a single 64-bit float format (`Rgba16Float`) for every step, HDR or not, is therefore only right for the HDR case** — under standard rendering the original uses far lower precision (16-bit, then 32-bit for the last pair), never a float format of any width.
- **The stored colour's channel order, reconfirmed independently.** `efxBloom`'s own final composite stage unpacks the renderable's colour (`.3`) by shifting out bits 24–31 as alpha, 16–23 as red, 8–15 as green and 0–7 as blue before scaling each by the camera fade — an independent confirmation (binary, medium), from a different routine than T01-417's field-copy reading, of "colours are stored ARGB" (`0xAARRGGBB`) above.
- **The three-anchor colour ramp's interpolation is CPU work, not a GPU pixel program** — a refinement of T01-424's "a third, separate pixel pass ... piecewise-linearly interpolates" phrasing. Reading `efxBloom` in full shows the two applicable anchors are selected and linearly blended by `.18` entirely in scalar float arithmetic, **before** any pixel program for this stage is bound; the GPU pass that follows only multiplies the already-resolved, single tint colour (now a plain constant) onto the downscaled image. The renderer's own `ramp_colour` function, which performs the same two-anchor linear interpolation in Rust rather than a shader, matches this placement.
- **The ramp's own component order (RGBA, BGRA or other) — narrowed, not proven.** The CPU interpolation carries each anchor's four stored components straight through by index (component 0 of each anchor feeds slot 0 of the blended result, and so on; no reordering appears anywhere in that arithmetic), and the result is handed to the GPU pixel program's constant register in that same index order. This is consistent with — though not a direct read of a hardware channel label for — the same RGBA order the composite stage's own colour unpack uses (previous point): a `BGRA` reading would make the ramp's dominant first component (≈0.66) blue, which does not fit the already-documented "warm, reddish-orange" description of that anchor. **Medium-low confidence for RGBA**, corroborating rather than independently proving T01-424's description.
- **The high-pass pass's own component order stays open**, narrowed only slightly: its pixel-program constants are built from two four-float groups, one holding three *separately*-sourced per-slot constants (plus a fixed 0.5 bias), the other holding the *same* single scale value copied into all three slots (plus one further HDR-dependent value) — a shape consistent with a per-channel luminance-style weighting in the first group against a channel-symmetric scale in the second, but this task could not read the first group's three source constants' literal values from the decompiled text (they are named data references, not inlined immediates, and reading them would mean dumping the module's data section rather than its code — out of scope here under the no-addresses rule). Whether the high-pass genuinely treats the three colour channels unequally (making its component order matter) or applies the same weight to each (making the order moot) is left for a task with the T11-008/T01-039 instrumentation harness (reading the constants at run time) rather than more static reading.

### Which models use the water shader, per level (T01-070, data script, high confidence)

A model uses the water shader when one of its mesh surfaces' material (`CRenderMeshSurface.16`, `bmf.md`) is, directly or through an external `.mtr`, a `CShaderPreset` whose `CShaderConfig.2` is a `CWaterShaderArgs`. `tools/re/water_models_verify.py` checks this for every `CStaticModelEntity` of every world (read-only, `SS2_DATA_DIR`, a few minutes):

- **27 of the 59 worlds** place at least one static model instance whose material resolves to one of those files. ## Underwater and fog overlays: where they come from (T01-070)

Confirming the existing finding that **neither is stored in the world**: no entity class in `environment-schema.md` carries an "underwater" or "fog overlay" record, and `CFluidHull::Render` is editor-only geometry (above). Both are runtime rules:

- **The global, screen-wide fog** that every material's vertex program applies (the "fog scale and bias for opaque and for translucent objects", `shaders.md`) is a classic **linear D3D fog**: `SeriousEngine::shaEnableFog(colour, near, far)` / `shaGetFogFactors` (`Engine.dll`, read in full, medium) store a colour and a near/far pair, clamp `far ≥ near + (a small engine constant)`, and compute `scale = 1/(far − near)`, `bias = −far × scale` for the non-blended case — the exact linear-fog formula `shaders.md` independently names from the shipped shader assembly's own constant-register documentation, a cross-check between two unrelated sources. **What calls `shaEnableFog`, with which near/far/colour, is now traced (T01-412, "Combining several hazes into one screen fog" above): `CViewRenCmd::ActivateExecution` calls it once per view per frame from the same per-frame haze draw-command list, combining every contributing haze by a fade-weighted average** (not "the nearest haze" nor a level-wide default), and the fog it sets up is depth-based fixed-function fog, not a per-pixel haze shader — see that section for the full formula, confidence and open details (the colour/alpha channel-to-accumulator mapping, the sky's fog status, and the clip-plane interaction all stay open).
- **Underwater state is a per-frame physics touch callback, not a stored flag.** `SeriousEngine::CLeggedPuppetEntity::OnFluidTouched(ownHull, otherHull, overlapAmount)` (`Sam2Game.dll`, read in full, medium) is the legged-puppet (player/NPC) collision callback fired when its body touches a `CFluidHull`. It stores `overlapAmount` (how much of the puppet's own collision volume currently overlaps the touched hull) in one field, and — only if the touched hull's dynamic type derives from the expected class — copies one float straight out of the touched hull object into a second field; that float, by `CFluidHull`'s member layout, is the hull's own density copy (`CFluidHull.1`: 1000, 500 or 980, the same value `wld-physics.md`/this spec's "Water" section already reports from the data). When nothing is touched, the second field is set to 0. So a puppet's own "am I in a fluid, and which one" state is **(overlap fraction, density-or-zero)**, refreshed every physics tick from collision, not read from the world file; `CPropEntity`/`CFlamerProjectileEntity`/the generic `CBaseEntity` have their own, separate `OnFluidTouched` overrides (not read by this task) for non-puppet objects (debris, projectiles).

- **Nothing reads these two fields to trigger a screen-wide overlay (T01-413).** Every reference to `CLeggedPuppetEntity`'s overlap-fraction and density fields across the fully decompiled `Sam2Game.dll` was listed exhaustively (binary, **medium**): both are read only inside `CLeggedPuppetEntity`'s own movement and footstep-sound logic — `GetPuppetMovingSoundRatio`, `StartMovingSound`/`StartNotMovingSound` (footstep sound set), `OnAnimEventStep` (splash sounds on an animation event) and `LerpToDesiredVelocityAndLook` (swim-speed damping) — and its own edge-triggered `OnStartedDiving`/`OnStoppedDiving` hooks, fired once per crossing of a single overlap threshold in `OnStep` (entering/leaving a fluid plays a splash/bubble sound; this base-class behaviour is never overridden by `CPlayerPuppetEntity`). No HUD element, `CCameraEntity` method or `CPlayerPuppetEntity` render/overlay routine reads either field directly. The two genuine full-screen fade mechanisms this task found — `CCameraEntity::RenderView`'s single-value fader (a `gfuFill` quad drawn right after the 3-D scene renders, whenever a per-camera float differs from its neutral value of 1.0) and `CPlayerPuppetEntity::RenderOverlayFadings`'s two fades — are keyed to an unrelated item/pickup "brain" flash timer and to `CWorldInfoEntity::OnSeriousBombExploded`'s own timestamp (the Serious Bomb weapon's screen flash), not to submersion. The Air HUD bar (`CAirSimpleHudElement`) is the only consumer found of a *derived* dive signal, and even it reads a coarser per-species move-state value (one of several values a generic move-type field takes) plus its own, separately tracked elapsed-dive timer — not the raw overlap/density pair.
- **So, within this decompile, there is no dedicated underwater screen-tint feature.** Whatever colour change the original shows underwater most plausibly comes from the same per-frame haze/fog mechanism already documented above — a haze volume authored to coincide with the fluid's own box would produce exactly that effect through the ordinary mechanism, with no fluid-specific code needed — not from a water-specific overlay. A reader should not build a separate "underwater tint" system distinct from the ordinary haze fog, and should drive any breath/air HUD element from the puppet's own fluid-overlap fraction crossing the same threshold the original uses for `OnStartedDiving`/`OnStoppedDiving` (an engine-side constant, not read off exactly by this task), not from a screen overlay.

## Per level

`environment-levels.md` (generated) has four tables: the sky (texture, size, viewer offset, colour), every haze (colour and the members above), every bloom, and per world the fluids (density, second value, box), force fields, shakers, damagers and portals. ## Reader invariants

1. Read the sky, haze, bloom and fluid values from the **property records**; the renderables repeat them.
2. A world without a sky entity, haze or bloom simply has none: do not substitute defaults from another level.
3. A haze is a box in world coordinates; several can overlap; their order is the list order (`CRenderingDomain.9` lists the haze renderables).
4. A fluid's box and density come from the hull; the visual water comes from materials, not from the fluid entity.
5. Fail on an unknown environment class; the classes in `environment-schema.md` are the complete list of the retail data.
6. **The sky cube's six face members are fixed directions, not an arbitrary list** (T01-405): `.10` North (−Z), `.11` West (−X), `.12` South (+Z), `.13` East (+X), `.14` Floor (−Y) and `.15` Ceiling (+Y); place each face's quad accordingly and ignore `CCubeBackgroundProperties.1` (it duplicates one of the six, not a seventh texture). A sky with five faces is missing the floor (`.14`) but has a ceiling; render it as an open bottom (no quad), never a default or repeated texture — now confirmed as the engine's own behaviour, not just a guess (T01-413). A sky with **no** face textures at all (T01-413: `1_4_approachingcity`, `dm_cockaleekie`, `dm_hole`) does **not** get an open shape: every face, floor included, renders as a flat quad in the sky's own tint colour (default white) — see "Faces with no texture" above for the exact per-face rule.
7. **The sky cube is rotated by `CCubeBackgroundEntity.en_qvPlacement`'s full rotation, around the camera, and never translated by its position** (T01-409): apply the rotation to the six face directions above before placing them around the viewer, offset vertically by `en_fViewerY`; `en_qvPlacement`'s position is not used by the renderer (evidence above) and must be ignored.
8. **A haze volume's visible strength depends on the camera, every frame** (T01-070): compute the camera's distance to the box inset by `.5` (in the box's own rotated frame) and scale the haze's contribution by `fade = 1 − clamp(distance / max(.5, 0.001), 0, 1)`; do not render a haze at full strength regardless of camera position, and do not skip this even when `.5` is authored 0 (treat it as 0.001, a near-hard cut). Clamp `.7` to `max(.6, 0.01, .7)` before using it. Decode the haze colour's alpha byte; it is read by the engine, even though this spec does not establish what it does with it.
9. **Neither the underwater state nor any fog/haze overlay is level data**: derive "is the camera submerged" from the same collision test the original uses (overlap with a `CFluidHull`, carrying that hull's density), not from a world-file flag; derive the screen-wide fog range/colour from the active haze(s) as **one linear, depth-based fog per frame**, over every haze whose `fade` (invariant 8) is non-zero this frame, **not a single "strongest haze" pick** (T01-412, "Combining several hazes" above). **The weight is not plain `fade_i`** (T01-415, "The exact per-channel arithmetic" above): each entry's `fade` is first scaled by this frame's largest contributing haze's own box-diagonal length divided by that entry's own — `weight_i = fade_i · maxDiagonal / diagonal_i` — before being summed; `near = Σ(weight_i·.6_i)/Σweight`, `far = Σ(weight_i·.7_i)/Σweight`, and likewise for the RGBA colour (alpha included, averaged the same way as red/green/blue, all four packed alpha-high into the one colour argument). This reduces to a plain fade-weighted average only when every contributing haze shares the same box diagonal (trivially true for one haze); with several simultaneous hazes of different box sizes, a smaller box outweighs a larger one at equal fade. **Separately**, the plain, unweighted `Σfade` (no diagonal scaling) still gates a width-only widen rule: when `Σfade < 1`, the near/far *already computed above* are further scaled by `Σfade` (near down, far up) — `near_final = Σfade · near_avg`, `far_final = far_avg / Σfade` — exactly as before; this check is unaffected by the diagonal weighting. **There is no separate underwater screen-tint system to replicate** (T01-413): the haze fog above is the only screen-wide colour effect this decompile found tied to the camera's position; drive any breath/air HUD element purely from the overlap fraction crossing the same threshold the original uses for `OnStartedDiving`/`OnStoppedDiving`, not from a screen overlay.
10. **The sky cube is drawn through a path that never itself arms or disarms fog — but across the install, that rarely leaves it unfogged** (T01-412, T01-416, T01-422, T01-428): do not apply the per-haze fog formula to the sky by the same code path a material would use, but do not default to "the sky is never fogged" either. The GPU's actual fog render state is armed or disarmed only by `shaGetFogFactors` (called once per material draw), not by `shaEnableFog` itself (which only updates a logical per-view flag), and every view disarms it unconditionally at its own end — so whether the sky inherits a fogged state turns on whether a fogged material drew earlier in the same view's sorted render-command list. T01-422 settled the sort mechanism at instruction level: the sky's own command sorts with ordinary near/opaque draws, never with the back-to-front/fading group a translucent or dithering-out material uses, tie-breaking on ascending camera distance to its own stored, largely arbitrary placement (T01-409), not the camera-recentred position it is actually drawn at. **T01-428 measured that distance against real camera positions across all 58 sky worlds** (`tools/re/sky_sort_distance_verify.py`, data, high confidence): the sky's own tie-break distance (11.3 to 53,200.4 world units, median 1,316.7) is reliably far larger than nearby ordinary opaque geometry's own real camera distance (0.3 to 53.0, median 5.6) — the nearest static model alone already beats the sky's distance in 226 of 227 sampled start/spawn camera positions across the whole install, with the sole near-miss (`dm_yodeller`) still losing at 8 of its own 9 cameras. So the original almost always arms the hardware fog bit, via an ordinary opaque draw, before the sky's own command executes. **Treat the sky as receiving the same per-frame fog (near/far/colour) as ordinary opaque geometry whenever a haze is actively contributing that frame** (invariants 8/9: its combined `fade` is non-zero), not as categorically unfogged; whether the sky's own fixed `gfuUseSimpleShader` draw path actually renders that armed state visibly (its vertex program's own fog-register output was not re-examined by T01-428) is the one remaining link this spec does not close (open questions, below).
11. **A bloom volume's visible strength depends on the camera, every frame, exactly like haze** (T01-423): compute the camera's distance to the box (renderable `.1`) inset by `.2` (in the box's own rotated frame) and scale the bloom's contribution by `fade = 1 − clamp(distance / max(.2, 0.001), 0, 1)`; a camera outside the un-inset box at all gets no contribution (the cull `CEffectRenCmd::Prepare` performs before the fade is even computed). **Several bloom entities do not average or pick between each other**: run each one's own downscale→high-pass→blur→additive-composite pass independently and in sequence against the same frame buffer, not as a single combined set of constants — a later pass sees (and can re-brighten) an earlier pass's own result. **Two simultaneous bloom entities tie exactly in the engine's own per-view sort** (T01-422): use the level's own object-list order for which one runs first, since nothing in the original engine picks between them by box size, distance or any other per-entity field. The entity's own colour (`.3`) feeds only the final additive composite, scaled by `fade` (its alpha channel is **not** scaled by `fade`); a module-global bias setting (not level data) additionally scales every bloom entity's strength for the whole frame. **A renderer slice should, per call:** (a) skip entirely if the camera is outside the un-inset box, or if `fade = 0`, or if the current render target is smaller than 32×32; (b) compute `intensity = clamp(fade × renderable.4 × efx_fBloomBias, 0, 2)` (T01-424) and skip if it is below a small floor; (c) repeatedly box-downscale the current frame by 4× per axis, each iteration also accumulating two offset (`±1.5` working-texel) copies weighted by renderable `.6` split asymmetrically by renderable `.12`, until both dimensions are ≤128 texels; (d) run one high-pass pass reading renderable `.13` scaled by `sqrt(intensity)` (plus, only under HDR, renderable `.19`, a per-entity value that is always 3 in the shipped data); (e) run one colour-ramp tint pass selecting along the fixed three-anchor ramp by renderable `.18`; (f) additively composite the result onto the real frame buffer, tinted by the entity's own colour `.3` (RGB scaled by `fade` and clamped to 1, alpha unscaled), through a mask texture if one is bound (never, in retail: `.7` is null in all 34 instances).
