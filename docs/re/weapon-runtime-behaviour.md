#### 2.1 Reload rules, restated with the Uzi's leftover switch

Only `bwp_ctFiresToReload` > 0 makes `ShouldReload`/`DoReload` do anything, and that member is 0 in every file except the Colt's (6). The Uzi has its **own** `ShouldReload` override (`CUziWeaponEntity`) that additionally needs `en_bPlayingSam2Renovation` (a global switch whose identity was not read) and single player and more than 49 shots fired — but its base-class reload still needs `ctFiresToReload` > 0, which the Uzi's files do not have, so **the Uzi never reloads in the retail data regardless of that switch** (`weapon-classes.md` rule 8, inherited, **low** for the switch's identity). The zap gun keeps its own fired-bullet counter (`zgwp_ctMaxFirePowerWorthBullets`, section 6 of `projectile-launch.md`) that is compared the same way and is equally inert (`bwp_ctFiresToReload` 0 in all 34 files).

### 3. Damage types of the two projectile damage sets (**high**, new in this task)

A hitscan weapon's bullet carries one damage type (`en_dtDamageType`, set once at `OnCreate`, column "damage type" above). A **projectile** weapon's file (`SBaseProjectileParams`) was checked, by the script, for every field whose name starts `bp_dt` (the convention for a damage-type member) across **all 94 projectile files**, and found exactly one: `bp_dtDirectDamageType`. There is no second, "area damage type" field anywhere in the class. Combined with `projectile-classes.md`'s own reading of `OnExplode` (the direct hit and the area damage are two separate calls into the same damage-delivery path, one right after the other, both inside the same host-only block), the only consistent reading is: **a projectile's direct hit and its area (splash) damage are always the same `EDamageType`** — there is no mechanism in the data or the class layout for them to differ. This directly answers the roadmap line's "damage types ... of the two projectile damage sets": they are not two types, they are one type applied twice (once at the impact point via the direct-damage pair, once over the hot-spot/fall-off area via the area-damage pair).

Per-weapon types (from section 2): `DMT_IMPACT` (8) for the rocket launcher, grenade launcher, cannon and Klodovik gun; `DMT_PLASMA` (18) for the plasma rifle and the zap gun; `DMT_HEAT` (14) for the flamer. The hand grenade (not a weapon, thrown by the player puppet directly, `projectile-launch.md` section 7) is also `DMT_IMPACT` (8), confirmed by the same cross-check on `HandGrenade.ep`.

### 4. Damage fall-off (**medium** for the pair, **low/open** for the spatial curve)

`bp_fDamageHotspot`/`Max` and `bp_fDamageFalloff`/`Max` are, like direct and area damage, a (value at fire power 0, value at fire power 1) pair lerped by the launch fire power (`projectile-classes.md` section 4, confirmed again by the script's read of the same fields). What stays open, already flagged by `projectile-classes.md`'s own open question 1 without a task ID, is the **spatial** shape of the fall-off within one explosion: full area damage is applied inside the hot-spot radius and none beyond hot-spot + fall-off, but the curve between those two radii (linear in distance is the natural reading of a "radius, then another radius" pair, but this was not confirmed instruction-by-instruction) and the unit the two numbers are in were not re-derived in this task either — they need either a cleaner disassembly pass (as `weapon-timing.md` open question 1 and `weapon-view.md` section 6 needed for their own unresolved arithmetic) or a measurement against the running game. This task does not mint a new ID for it: it is the natural companion measurement for the existing **T01-433** oracle capture (fire a rocket at increasing distance from a damageable target and record how the damage drops, if the harness can read a target's health) or a future instruction-level follow-up of **T01-131**, whichever runs first.

### 5. Victim kinds (assembled from existing pages; **medium**, inherited)

Which entities a hit actually reaches is a property of the entity *hit*, not of the weapon:

- **Puppets** (players and NPCs). `ReceivesDamageFrom` (`puppet-entity-methods.md`): a player-controlled puppet hit by another player-controlled puppet takes it always in deathmatch, otherwise only with friendly fire on (`sgp_bFriendlyFire`); a puppet that is **not** evil (good, semi-neutral, or a player) takes damage only from an **evil**-aligned dealer; one more identity test (a dealer with the same value of an unresolved base-entity member) is refused unless the hit is an explosion. Once a hit passes that test, `OnReceiveDamage` (`puppet-entity-classes.md` section 7) lets the controlling player brain's god cheat or invulnerability block it — **except** a maximum-value hit (2,147,483,647) of a type other than the kill-all cheat (`DMT_KILL_ALL_CHEAT`, 22) or the Serious Bomb (`DMT_SERIOUS_BOMB`, 23), which still goes through; the level's boss puppet additionally ignores any maximum-value hit outright. A hit on a mechanism part that carries a shield tool (`CShieldDescription`) is redirected to the tool's own health instead of the puppet's. The health/armour arithmetic, the kick and the score that follow are **T01-140**, not this task.
- **Destructible world-content models and props** (`worldcontent-models-props.md`). Their own `OnReceiveDamage` is gated by `ModelFlags`, not by alignment: `MF_IGNORE_DAMAGE` blocks everything while a destruction is already running, and kicks (damage type 7, `DMT_KICKING`) are separately gated by `MF_IGNORE_KICKING_DAMAGE`/`MF_IGNORE_NON_KICKING_DAMAGE`; what gets through is handed to the model's own destruction rules (`ProcessModelReceiveDamage`), not to a health member.
- **A handful of mechanisms with their own health pool**: the bouncer's diamond hand (`bouncer-motor-joint-rules.md`, max 500, ignores damage type 7 and any hit from an evil-aligned dealer) and the Kleer boss-arena hand chain (`mechanism-classes-movers.md`, 500 health each, what happens at 0 "not established").
- **Physical generic projectiles** can themselves be hit (`projectile-classes.md`, `CGenericProjectileEntity::OnReceiveDamage`): a `PN_PHYSICAL` one with positive health loses that health and is destroyed at 0; a magic one, or one with health −1, ignores every hit — this is how, for example, a rocket can be shot out of the air.

So "victim kind" is not a property the weapon's own data ever encodes (no weapon or projectile file names an allowed victim kind); it is entirely a property of the thing hit, read above from four different `OnReceiveDamage` overrides.

### 6. The Serious Bomb (**medium**, inherited; the damage amount is open, T01-120)

Pressing fire pays one `AI_SERIOUS_BOMB` unit (cap 3, section 2) through the ordinary `OnFire` chain, then `WS_WAIT_FUSE_TO_BURN` instead of `WS_RECOIL` — the **only** state `CanBeChanged` refuses, so the player cannot switch away while the fuse burns (`weapon-timing.md` section 1's state table; `weapon-classes.md` rule 9). The fuse's own animation length is the state's duration; there is no stored data member for it, unlike every weapon's recoil. At the end of the fuse, `Explode` applies a world effect to every puppet in the world-info entity's own list that is in state 1 (read as "alive"), raises the replicated remote call `OnSeriousBombExploded` on the world-info entity and tells the player `OnUsedSeriousBomb`, then returns to idle. **The effect's damage amount and which victim kinds it excludes were not read** — `weapon-classes.md` already says so explicitly, and closing it is **T01-120**'s job (which depends on this task and on `weapon-classes.md` already). The beam gun is in a different position: its damage **is** in the data (`bwp_slDamage`, `.ep` member `.1`, 14 single player / 7 deathmatch, `weapon-classes.md` section 4.3), only the tick function `DoDamage` that applies it was not read; the circular saw's cutting damage (`en_fCuttingDamagePerSec`) is, like the bomb's, a plain entity member with **no** value anywhere in the data.

### 7. Combo weapons and dual wielding (**medium**, inherited; fully answered by `weapon-pickups.md` section 5, T01-122)

Summary for this checklist: the combo system (`ggp_cwuComboWeaponsUsing` = `CWU_ALLOW_CUSTOM`, 2, in the data) is switched **off** by the default session option `sgp_bUseComboWeapons` (0), and no world places the combo pickup — a default game never offers it. With the option on, `ToggleDualWielding` lets a player hold two weapons: by default only the Uzi or the Colt doubled (the inventory's separate "doubled" bit set defaults to {Uzi, Colt}, `samGetDefaultDoubleWeaponFlags`), or, through the combo pickup, any two different weapons. While dual-wielding **with** combo enabled, `CanFireFromWeaponNow` always allows firing, so the two hands act independently; with the option off, the (necessarily equal) pair alternates, delayed by the firing weapon's own `bwp_fDoubleWeaponFireDelay` (section 2's weapons do not list it above since it does not change which weapon fires or its damage, only the second hand's timing).

### 8. Weapon switching order

Two different orders answer to this name:

1. **The strength order** (`samGetWeaponStrengthIndex`, `weapon-pickups.md` section 2, **medium**, inherited): a fixed ranking of all 17 indices, weakest first — saw, Colt, zap gun, auto shotgun, double shotgun, Uzi, minigun, rocket launcher, grenade launcher, plasma rifle, beam gun, sniper, Klodovik gun, cannon, Serious Bomb, combo weapons, flamer. It is read only when a weapon is received and the auto-change setting is "only if stronger" (`ACW_ONLY_IF_STRONGER`): the newly received weapon replaces the right-hand weapon only when its rank is higher (or the right hand is empty); setting "on" always switches, "off" never does (`weapon-pickups.md` section 3).
2. **The next/previous/favourite/strongest weapon cycle** (`FindNextWeaponThatCanBeUsed`, `FindPreviousWeaponThatCanBeUsed`, `FindFavoriteWeaponThatCanBeUsed`, `GetStrongestWeaponThatCanBeUsed`, named but not read in `players-session-classes.md` section 3.1) is a **different, still unread** rule — nothing here says it follows the strength order above, and it is explicitly left to **T01-114**.
3. The base weapon entity's own `GetPreferredNextWeapon` — what a weapon suggests switching to once it can no longer be used, e.g. out of ammunition — returns the literal "none" in the base class (`weapon-classes.md` section 6.1, read), and none of the 17 weapon entities override it (section 6.2's hook lists). So the retail game never auto-suggests a replacement weapon through this hook; whatever happens when a weapon runs dry goes through the broader weapon-selection rules of point 2, not this one.

## Test vectors

All from the retail install via `tools/re/weapon_runtime_verify.py` for the data, combined with the rules of `weapon-timing.md`/`projectile-launch.md`/`hitscan-shot.md` for the formulas; **nothing run** on the original (T01-433 is where that happens).

1. **Colt, six shots then a seventh.** The Colt needs **no ammunition kind** (cost 0, cap 0 — it is simply always usable) but still limits itself to **6 shots per magazine** (`bwp_ctFiresToReload` = 6, the only weapon with a magazine in the retail data). Every shot: fire power 0 (never charges), so the fire period is `lerp(0.5, 0, 0)` = **0.5 s** exactly; damage **10** (deathmatch **15**) per hit, type `DMT_BULLET` (19); with the serious-damage power-up active in single player, a hit deals `trunc(10 × 4.0)` = **40**. After the sixth shot's 0.5 s recoil ends, `ShouldReload()` is true (6 shots fired since the last reload ≥ `ctFiresToReload`): the entity enters `WS_RELOAD` instead of `WS_IDLE`, the shot counter resets to 0, and because the Colt's `bwp_fReloadDuration` is 0 the reload animation plays at its own natural length (not rescaled).
2. **Auto shotgun, one shot, point-blank.** Costs **1 shell** (cap 200); fire period `lerp(1.0, 0, 0)` = **1.0 s** single player (deathmatch **0.819 s**, since the deathmatch file's own minimum recoil is 0.819, not lerped from the single-player number); fires **7 pellets** of **12** damage each (deathmatch **10**), type `DMT_SLUG` (21): **84** total at point-blank range (deathmatch **70**) if every pellet hits, scattered over a cone of radius 0 to 0.5 at 100 units' distance (deathmatch: radius 0 to 3 at 500 units). With the serious-damage power-up: `trunc(12 × 4.0)` = **48** per pellet, **336** total.
3. **Minigun, held fire from cold.** Costs **1 bullet per shot** (cap 1000, never reloads). The barrel accelerates from 0 to 550°/s (deathmatch 500°/s) over 0.5 s; only once it reaches that speed exactly does `OnStep` start firing, every `lerp(0.035, 0, 0)` = **0.035 s** (deathmatch 0.1 s) — about 28.6 rounds/s single player. Each bullet: **10** damage (deathmatch **12**), type `DMT_BULLET` (19), scattered over radius 0 to 1 at 200 units (deathmatch: radius 0 to 22.5 at 750 units). Releasing the trigger decelerates the barrel back to 0 over 1.5 s regardless of how long it was held.
4. **Rocket launcher, one shot.** Costs **1 rocket** (cap 50, never reloads); fire power passed to the projectile is always the literal 0, so the launched rocket always uses the **power-0** pair of its file regardless of anything the player does: fire period `lerp(0.6, 0, 0)` = **0.6 s** (deathmatch **0.665 s**); direct damage **50**, area (splash) damage **80** (deathmatch: area **50**, direct unchanged at 50) — both numbers share one damage type, `DMT_IMPACT` (8) (section 3). With the serious-damage power-up active in single player: direct `trunc(50 × 4.0)` = **200**, area `trunc(80 × 4.0)` = **320** at the blast centre, falling toward 0 somewhere beyond the hot-spot radius (4.0, unit open, section 4) by the fall-off distance (10.0, same open unit).
